# Composition Canon 06 — Visual Rhythm and Sectioning

Domain: how adjacent zones of one continuous surface relate and separate WITHOUT boxes — the section-separation ladder, vertical rhythm, section header grammar, dividers and tint zones, alignment grammar, scale-as-sectioning, continuity/atmosphere between zones, and the rules that make a screen read as one composition instead of a stack of glued-together modules. This canon operationalizes the north star's "semantic zones" direction (`design-north-star-2026-07-16.md`).

Element-level law lives in UX Canon 04 (`audits/workout-overhaul-2026-07-22/ux-canon/04-visual-motion-content.md`): the type scale and all-caps label mechanics are §1 (#13, #17), the spacing scale, proximity rule, alignment-edge rule, and section-spacing token are §2 (#21, #23, #24, #28, #33, #36), and emphasis/focal rules are §4 (#50–#60). This file does not restate them; it says how to compose with them.

Tag key: **[mechanical]** = checkable by rule/tool with no taste required. **[judgment]** = requires a human (or design-trained) eye, but the direction of "better" is agreed.

Sources abbreviated: RUI = Refactoring UI (Wathan/Schoger); M3 = Material Design 3 (incl. 2025 "Expressive" update); HIG = Apple Human Interface Guidelines; NN/g = Nielsen Norman Group (incl. their Gestalt series); VWIG = Vercel Web Interface Guidelines / vercel.com design practice; JMB = Josef Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems in Graphic Design*, and the editorial-grid tradition descending from it; Gestalt = the perception principles (proximity, similarity, continuity, common region, figure-ground) as documented by NN/g and Interaction Design Foundation; Precedent = direct study of shipped cardless surfaces (Stripe dashboard post-2024 card-less system, Linear, Things 3, Apple Fitness/Health); NS = the owner's design north star (2026-07-16). Canon 04 = the ux-canon file above.

A plain-words Gestalt glossary, since these terms recur: **proximity** = things close together are read as one group; **similarity** = things that look alike are read as related even when apart; **continuity** = the eye follows lines and edges and assumes aligned things belong to one flow; **common region** = things inside the same drawn boundary (a card, a box) are read as a group — this is the one principle the north star retires as the default; **figure-ground** = at any moment one thing reads as "the subject" and everything else reads as "the background," and the eye needs that split to be unambiguous.

## 1. The Section-Separation Ladder

How to draw a boundary between two zones without a box. The canonical ladder is stated ONCE, in comp-canon 01 #11; this section is its zone-level application, using 01's rung numbers. *(patched 2026-07-23: this file's parallel six-rung cumulative ladder deleted; unified with 01 #11)*

1. **Use the canonical separation ladder (01 #11), always climbing from the bottom: (0) shared flow → (1) whitespace step → (2) alignment shift/indent → (3) section header → (4) hairline divider → (5) background-tint zone, whose named zone-level variant is the atmosphere/color-family shift (#38–39) → (6) bordered container → (7) elevated card → (8) separate surface** — each rung is a *substitute* for the ones below at that boundary, never an addition to them (#4); a boundary uses the weakest rung that makes the grouping unmistakable, and rungs 6+ exist only under the north star's escape hatch ("genuinely no other way") gated by 01 §4's earning tests. An alignment shift (rung 2) is a real separator at zone level too: a switch from the centered identity lockup to the left spine marks "the intro is over" with zero ink. [comp-canon 01 #11; RUI, NS, Gestalt] [judgment] *(patched 2026-07-23: renumbered to 01 #11's rungs; alignment rung added; cumulative framing deleted)*
2. **Whitespace is the default separator; reach for anything stronger only when whitespace demonstrably fails** — a bigger gap is the cheapest boundary there is: it adds zero ink, zero elements, and zero maintenance. Vercel's own site separates major sections almost entirely with vertical space; Things 3 separates task groups with nothing but gaps and headers. If a session's first instinct at a boundary is a line or a tint, the instinct is wrong. [RUI, VWIG, Precedent (Things 3, Vercel)] [judgment]
3. **Whitespace fails, and you escalate a rung, in exactly these cases: (a) the gap needed would exceed the macro step of the spacing scale, (b) the two zones are visually similar enough to smear together (two lists, two prose blocks), or (c) the surface scrolls and a zone boundary must survive being seen only partially** — these are the conditional triggers; escalating without one of them is decoration. Case (c) matters most on phones: mid-scroll, a pure-gap boundary can read as "the page ended." [RUI, NN/g, Precedent (iOS settings-style inset lists exist for case b/c)] [judgment]
4. **One boundary, one rung — never stack redundant separators** — a big gap PLUS a divider PLUS a tint change at the same boundary is triple-drawn; it reads as nervous and re-creates the heaviness of cards by accumulation. Pick the rung; delete the rest. (A section header on top of a whitespace step is the one sanctioned pair — the header is labeling, not separating.) [RUI ("don't use borders and shadows together" generalized), VWIG] [mechanical]
5. **Use the same rung for the same kind of boundary everywhere in the app** — if peer zones on Home separate by whitespace + header, peer zones on History separate by whitespace + header too. The inconsistency this canon exists to kill is precisely "sometimes a gap, sometimes a line, sometimes a box" for the same structural relationship. Map each boundary type (zone↔zone, group↔group inside a zone, item↔item) to one rung once, in tokens/components, and reuse. [DS consensus, Canon 04 §2 #33, NS goal 3 (one coherent visual model)] [mechanical]
6. **A boundary's strength must match the semantic distance it marks** — zones that answer different user questions (today's workout vs. sleep) deserve a stronger boundary than groups inside one answer (sets within an exercise). If a weak semantic seam gets a strong visual separator, the screen fragments; if a strong seam gets a weak one, zones smear. Rank the seams on a screen before styling any of them. [Gestalt (proximity encodes relatedness), RUI, JMB] [judgment]

## 2. Vertical Rhythm — the Spacing Beat

7. **The same structural relationship always gets the same gap, on every screen** — define the vocabulary once (e.g. item↔item within a group; group↔group within a zone; header↔its content; zone↔zone) and bind each to one spacing token. A reader can't articulate why a screen feels sloppy when identical relationships get different gaps, but they feel it instantly. This is the single highest-leverage rule in this file. [RUI, JMB (the grid as repeated modular intervals), Canon 04 §2 #21/#33] [mechanical]
8. **Unequal-on-purpose gaps are the carrier of structure — THE canonical gap table, token-anchored: item↔item within a group = `--sp-2`–`--sp-3` (8–12px); group↔group within a zone = `--sp-4`–`--sp-5` (16–24px); zone↔zone = `--zone-gap` (56px at phone widths → 96px desktop)** — the eye groups by relative, not absolute, distance (proximity, in plain words: whatever sits closest together is read as belonging together). If zone gaps are 32 and item gaps are 24, the 1.3× ratio is below perceptual threshold and the page reads as one undifferentiated column. The ≥2× jump between adjacent levels is the non-tunable floor, and it binds the values actually used on a screen: if items run at 12, groups run at ≥24. This table is stated once, here; 01 #22, 02 #20/#35, and 05 #3 cite it rather than restating numbers. [Gestalt/NN/g proximity, RUI, VWIG (64px+ major-section padding observed); APPROVED ds.css `--sp` scale + `--zone-gap`] [mechanical] *(patched 2026-07-23: declared canonical; zone beat re-anchored to --zone-gap 56–96)*
9. **Never separate two zones with a gap that also occurs inside a zone** — if 24px appears both between two stat rows and between the hydration zone and the sleep zone, the boundary is ambiguous by construction. The spacing levels must be disjoint sets, not overlapping ranges. This is greppable: audit rendered gaps per relationship type. [Gestalt, RUI] [mechanical]
10. **Do the gaps-only audit before shipping: blur or strip the content and look at nothing but the vertical intervals** — squint at the screenshot (or literally blur it 8px). The grouping structure — which things clump, where the seams fall — must survive with all text unreadable. If the blurred page reads as one even smear, rhythm has failed regardless of how good the components look. [RUI (squint test), JMB, NN/g] [judgment]
11. **The vertical beat holds across scroll, not just within a viewport** — a long surface is experienced one viewport at a time; if the zone beat is 64 in the first screenful and drifts to 40/72/56 further down, the lower page feels like a different app. Spot-check gaps at the bottom of the page as rigorously as the top. [JMB, Precedent (Linear's settings and issue views hold one beat for their whole height)] [mechanical]
12. **Whitespace belongs to content, not to leftover room: assign every gap an owner** — implement gaps as the structural relationship's token (stack gap, section margin), never as ad-hoc padding tweaked per element until it "looks right." Ad-hoc padding is how the arbitrary-gap disease enters a codebase. [RUI, DS consensus, Canon 04 §2 #21] [mechanical]
13. **Density may shift between zones, but the beat may not** — a data-dense zone (set table) can run a tighter item beat than an airy zone (today's focus), and that contrast is expressive and welcome — but both zones still sit on the same zone↔zone beat, and each zone uses one density throughout itself. Density variation inside a shared rhythm reads as intentional composition; rhythm variation reads as error. [Canon 04 §2 #34, JMB, NS (shifts in visual density listed as a grouping tool)] [judgment]

## 3. Section Header Grammar

14. **A section header binds to its content by asymmetric spacing: visibly more space above the header than below it** — the header is the first line of the section it labels, not a divider floating between sections. House default: space-above ≈ the zone beat (`--zone-gap` — this is the zone boundary's cost, not the header's; 02 #23 budgets only the line + below-gap), space-below ≈ one group beat (12–16). A header centered in its gap is the single most common rhythm defect in shipped UI. Situational exception (owner ruling 2026-07-19, scoped per the never-generalize rule): the approved Home header identity lockup takes equal air date → headline → numeral — those elements are one unit, not a header labeling content — so this rule does not apply there, and no session may "fix" it. If a second such collision appears elsewhere, ask the owner rather than widening either rule. [Gestalt/NN/g proximity, RUI, BPT-style heading practice; wave0 decision log] [mechanical] *(patched 2026-07-23: equal-air Home lockup exception scoped; beat tokenized)*
15. **One header style per structural level, defined once** — zone headers, sub-group headers, and inline labels each get exactly one lockup (face, size, case, tracking, color) reused verbatim everywhere. The north star's editorial grammar assigns the spaced small-caps overline to labels (mechanics per Canon 04 §1 #17); the zone header level is where it lives. Caps live ONLY in the two approved eyebrow roles (`.t-label`/`.t-spec`, 1–3-word tracked micro-labels — Q-DS-4, owner 2026-07-17): no header level may set sentences, explanations, or multi-word aggregates in caps. Two different treatments for the same level on sibling screens is a defect even if both are pretty. [NS (editorial hierarchy), M3 type roles, DS consensus; Q-DS-4] [mechanical] *(patched 2026-07-23: Q-DS-4 caps scope bound to header levels)*
16. **Headers are quiet; content is loud** — the header is a wayfinding label, so it recedes: small, tracked, muted color. The zone's data (the display numeral, the scene, the workout name) carries the visual weight. A header set larger or darker than its own content inverts the hierarchy — the reader is served the filing label instead of the file. [RUI (labels de-emphasized — Canon 04 §4 #53), NS, Precedent (Apple Fitness: tiny "Activity" overline, enormous rings/numerals)] [mechanical]
17. **Omit the header when the content self-identifies — everywhere EXCEPT Home, where headers are never omitted** — a hydration scene organized around a jug does not need "HYDRATION" above it if the form is unmistakable; a header there is redundant noise. Keep headers where content is ambiguous (a bare list of numbers) or where the label is a scroll-scan waypoint on a long page. When in doubt on a long scrolling surface, keep it — headers are also the page's table of contents. On Home the section title IS the doorway (title + ›, owner decision 2026-07-20): it is functional navigation, not just a label, so it always renders. [RUI (drop needless labels — Canon 04 §4 #54), NN/g (scanning), NS (scene-based presentation); 2026-07-20 Home doorway decision] [judgment] *(patched 2026-07-23: Home carve-out per the doorway decision)*
18. **A header may carry the zone's one inline utility (a period switcher, a single named action link) on its baseline, and nothing more** — the header row is a legitimate home for the zone's single affordance, right-aligned on the same baseline, rendered in sentence case (never caps — Q-DS-4). Two or more controls in a header row turns the label into a toolbar and the zone into an app-within-an-app. On Home the convention is fixed by the 2026-07-20 doorway decision: the title itself (with ›) is the navigation, "See all"/"See X history" links do not exist there, and the one permitted utility is the section's single named action link (e.g. "Log water") opening the dedicated page scrolled to its log area. [HIG (section headers with trailing links), Precedent (Apple Health, App Store), M3; 2026-07-20 Home doorway decision, Q-DS-4] [mechanical] *(patched 2026-07-23: "See all" example replaced per the Home doorway decision)*
19. **Never let a header dangle: a header must have at least two lines of its own content visible beneath it before a page/scroll boundary** — a label with nothing under it (or one orphaned line) reads as a broken page. If layout forces a dangle at common heights, move the boundary, not the header. [editorial/print orphan-widow practice generalized, NN/g] [mechanical]

19a. **When a screen opens with the large editorial title, it collapses to a compact bar title on scroll — the standard iOS/M3 pattern** — the identity moment (the one header that earns real vertical space, 02 #23) lives in the first viewport at display scale; as the user scrolls, it pins as a one-line bar title with the on-scroll hairline/shadow cue (01 #20). Only the title and the screen's 1–2 header actions ride the collapsed bar; nothing else may join it mid-scroll, and the collapse must be the same choreography on every surface that uses it. Screens without an editorial title moment simply keep their compact bar throughout. [HIG (large titles), M3 (top app bar scroll behavior); 01 #20, 02 #23; added 2026-07-23 per completeness sweep THIN #10] [mechanical]

## 4. Hairline Dividers and Tint Zones

20. **When a divider earns its place (rung 4, 01 #11), it is a hairline: 1px (0.5px/hairline on 2× screens where the stack supports it), full-width of its list, at ~8–15% foreground opacity** — a divider is punctuation, not architecture. Vercel's practice (1px at rgba ~0.08) and iOS separators sit in this band; in ds.css, `--hairline` (.09) sits inside it and `--hairline-2` (.16) is the approved emphasis step one point over the stated band — the token wins. Anything 2px+ or high-contrast stops separating content and starts being content. [VWIG, HIG, RUI; APPROVED ds.css hairline tokens] [mechanical] *(patched 2026-07-23: rung renumbered to 01 #11; hairline tokens reconciled)*
21. **Divider inset carries meaning: inset = "the group continues," full-bleed = "the group ends"** — the iOS convention worth adopting: a divider indented to the content's left spine separates sibling items inside one group; a divider running edge-to-edge closes the group. Using them interchangeably wastes a channel and confuses the boundary strength. Pick the semantics once and encode them in the list component. [HIG, Precedent (iOS lists, Things 3)] [mechanical]
22. **Dividers separate repeated siblings, not arbitrary neighbors** — the legitimate divider habitat is a run of same-shaped rows (history entries, settings rows) where whitespace alone would either smear (too tight) or bloat (loose enough to separate). Between two unlike zones, a divider is the wrong tool — that seam belongs to the whitespace beat and header grammar. [RUI ("borders last resort" — Canon 04 §5 #66), Gestalt (similarity already relates the rows; the divider only marks the cut)] [judgment]
23. **A background-tint zone shift (rung 5, 01 #11) is one step on the surface ramp, applied full-bleed** — to mark a region by tint, shift the background a single token step (surface → surface-raised) and run the tinted band to the viewport edges. Full-bleed is what makes it read as a *region of the page* rather than an object sitting on the page. (DS gap, flagged 2026-07-23: ds.css currently ships only `--bg` — no surface ramp or tint-zone token exists; it is on the chadchat port session's list, and until it lands, rung-5 tints need an owner-approved token addition, not a per-screen hex.) [M3 surface-container ramp, VWIG ("subtle surface color changes to separate content, not divider lines"), Precedent (Stripe's card-less system)] [mechanical] *(patched 2026-07-23: rung renumbered; missing surface-ramp token flagged)*
24. **The moment a tinted region gains a radius, border, or shadow, it has become a card — stop** — tint + inset margins + rounded corners + elevation is the common-region container the north star retires. A tint zone stays a tint zone only while it is borderless, square-edged against the viewport, and shadowless. This is the bright line between rung 5 and rungs 6+ (01 #11; same line as 01 #32). [NS (anti-goal: swapping cards for other container shapes), Gestalt (common region), Canon 04 §5 #66; 01 #32] [mechanical] *(patched 2026-07-23: rung letters renumbered to 01 #11)*
25. **Alternating-tint striping of every section is banned; tint at most one or two regions per screen** — zebra-striping the whole page (band, band, band) is the "stack of unrelated modules" read with extra steps — it exposes the grid as the visual language, which the north star explicitly rejects. Tint is for singling out: the one zone in a different mode (the active workout), or a footer register change. [NS, JMB (the grid aligns, it doesn't display itself), Precedent (Linear tints only its active/selected contexts)] [judgment]

## 5. Alignment Grammar — the Spine

26. **A cardless surface hangs on one shared left spine: the single left edge that headers, body content, and data of every zone return to** — with no boxes to hold zones together vertically, the aligned edge is what makes ten zones read as one page (continuity, in plain words: the eye rides an unbroken line and binds everything on it into one flow). The spine equals the page margin token (Canon 04 §2 #28) and runs the full scroll height. Break it and the page falls apart into fragments — no other rule can save it. [JMB, Gestalt/NN/g continuity, RUI (few strong edges — Canon 04 §2 #24), Precedent (Things 3, Linear: everything returns to one edge)] [mechanical]
27. **Every additional left alignment line must buy its keep; budget ~2–3 per screen including the spine** — each new left edge (an indent level, a second column) is a structural claim the reader must parse. The reference mock resolves to about three macro alignment lines — that is the ceiling, not the floor. Elements that *almost* share an edge (off by 2–6px) are the worst case: they read as broken rather than as two lines. [RUI, JMB, Canon 04 §2 #24, NS reference mock] [mechanical]
28. **Indentation from the spine means "child of the thing above," and means nothing else** — one fixed indent step of `--sp-4` (16px) signals containment without a container: sets indent under their exercise, detail rows indent under their metric. Never indent for looks, and never express hierarchy by any other horizontal offset. One indent level is the phone default and two is the hard maximum anywhere (same law as 02 #15 — Canon 04 §2 #36's nesting cap, expressed as edges instead of boxes); deeper hierarchy is typography's job. [Gestalt (proximity + continuity standing in for common region), RUI, Precedent (Things 3 headings→tasks, code editors); 02 #15] [mechanical] *(patched 2026-07-23: step fixed at --sp-4; one-default/two-max aligned with 02 #15)*
29. **Right edges stay ragged except for data; numerals right-align to a shared right edge when they must be compared** — forcing every zone to justify to both margins produces stretched, gappy layouts (Canon 04 §2 #26). But a column of comparable numbers (weights, durations, totals) earns the one disciplined right edge, with tabular figures per Canon 04 §1 #11/#12. One spine left, at most one data edge right. [BPT/RUI, JMB] [mechanical]
30. **Align optically, not mechanically, at the spine** — large display numerals, quote marks, and round or pointed glyphs must slightly overhang the mechanical edge to *look* aligned on it; icons center to the text's cap height, not the box. The spine is a perceived line, and perception is the judge (Canon 04 §2 #32 applied to the page level). [RUI, HIG, editorial practice (hanging punctuation)] [judgment]
31. **Full-bleed elements must contain the spine's continuation** — when a scene or image band runs edge-to-edge between spined zones, its internal focal content (the jug, the headline over the image) sits on or references the spine, so the eye's line survives the interruption instead of restarting after it. This is how the reference mock's illustration bands stay part of the page. [JMB (grid governs images and text alike), NS reference mock, Gestalt continuity] [judgment]

31a. **In RTL locales the spine mirrors wholesale: the shared edge becomes a right spine, indent steps move leftward from it, and reading-gravity rules flip with it (08 #7a)** — the one deliberate non-mirroring case is numerals: Western digits keep their own alignment logic (a compared-numbers column still shares one disciplined edge, per #29), because digit order does not flip in RTL text. Element-level bidi mechanics (icons, text direction, punctuation) are ux-canon 04 §14; this rule owns only the screen-level spine and indent direction. Low urgency for the English-only launch, but binding on any localized build — the canon claims generality. [Material bidirectionality guidance, HIG right-to-left, ux-canon 04 §14; added 2026-07-23 per completeness sweep THIN #5] [mechanical]

## 6. Scale and Typography as Sectioning

32. **A decisive scale jump is itself a section boundary — when a zone opens with a display-scale element, the whitespace step above it can shrink** — a 48px serif numeral after 14px body text announces "new zone" louder than any divider; spending a full zone gap AND a display opener on the same seam over-separates. Scale contrast must be decisive to work alone: the display element ≥ ~2.5–3× body size (the north-star editorial pattern), not a timid 1.3×. [RUI (hierarchy before separators), NS (editorial hierarchy), JMB (scale contrast as structure)] [judgment]
33. **The display-numeral + small-caps-label lockup is the standard zone opener; build it once and reuse it** — the north star's approved pattern (spaced small-caps overline, large serif numeral/headline, quiet unit or context line) is this surface's equivalent of a card header. One shared component, one internal spacing, everywhere — its mechanics per Canon 04 §1 #13/#17 and the ds.css tokens. Zones that open with the same lockup are read as siblings by similarity (in plain words: repeating the same look tells the reader "these are the same kind of thing") — which is exactly how peer zones should read. [NS, Gestalt similarity, DS consensus] [mechanical]
34. **Reserve display scale for zone-level statements — one display element per zone, and not every zone gets one** — if every metric on screen is enormous, none is (Canon 04 §4 #51). Zones lower in the day's hierarchy open at headline scale, not display scale. The resulting size gradient down the page is itself information: the reader infers priority from the loudness map. [RUI, NS goal 1 (today's action understood in 5s), HIG] [judgment]
35. **Typeface role-switching marks register, not decoration: the serif display face signals "a measured result," the sans signals "interface"** — keeping the two faces' roles strict (per Canon 04 §1 #9/#10) means the face itself becomes a sectioning cue — a serif numeral is instantly "data about you," sans text is instantly "controls and context." Diluting the serif into buttons or labels destroys the signal. [NS typography direction, BPT via Canon 04, editorial practice] [mechanical]
36. **Similarity groups at a distance: elements sharing exact style read as one system even when separated** — the same muted small-caps style on every zone label threads the zones together across the whole scroll (this is Gestalt similarity working *for* coherence). The corollary is a trap: any two things styled identically WILL be read as the same kind of thing, so never reuse a zone-label style for a non-label, or a stat lockup for a button. [Gestalt/NN/g similarity, RUI] [judgment]

## 7. Continuity, Atmosphere, and Connective Tissue

37. **The background is one continuous field for the whole surface — zones never own rectangles of background** — one page-long background (flat, gradient, or atmospheric) that runs beneath all zones is the base layer that makes the page one thing. The moment each zone brings its own background rectangle, the layout has reinvented cards. Atmosphere is painted ONTO the shared field, not swapped per-zone. [NS ("the background runs continuously"), Precedent (reference mock), Gestalt figure-ground] [mechanical]
38. **Zone atmospheres shift by gradient hand-off, never by hard horizontal seam** — where the hydration blues give way to sleep purples, the color families cross-fade over a tall transition band (≥ the zone gap in height), so the boundary is felt, not drawn. A hard-edged color stop is a divider in costume — it re-cuts the page into bands. [NS (each zone's atmosphere "bleeds slightly into the next"), reference mock] [judgment]
39. **Atmosphere hue may encode zone identity, but structure must survive in greyscale** — tinting the field toward a zone's family (blue = hydration, violet = sleep) is welcome connective tissue, but it is an *enhancement channel*: the grouping must still be legible from spacing, headers, and alignment alone (Canon 04 §3 #42/#48 applied to composition). Users with low color vision get the same page structure. [NS, WCAG-derived practice, Canon 04 §3] [mechanical]
40. **Bridge deliberate elements across seams to stitch zones together** — a scene element that breaks the boundary (mist from the sleep zone drifting up into the gap; the spine-riding illustration overlapping the gap between two zones by a controlled amount) tells the eye the zones share one world. Use sparingly — one or two bridges per screen — and never let a bridge obscure content or a tap target. [NS (connective tissue), editorial layout practice (images crossing columns in JMB tradition)] [judgment]
41. **Kill the module-stack read: adjacent zones must differ in at least one of height, internal density, or internal layout** — a page of equal-height, same-layout bands reads as tiles without borders — the disease with the symptom hidden. The reference mock alternates: a tall illustrated scene, then a compact two-column arrangement, then a horizontal band. If two adjacent zones came out the same shape, restructure one (or accept they're true siblings and make them ONE zone with two groups). [NS goal 3 + anti-goals, JMB (varied module combinations on one grid), Precedent (Stripe, Apple Health mix row-, hero-, and grid-shaped sections)] [judgment]
42. **Every screen region has exactly one figure; the rest of the region is ground** — figure-ground in plain words: the eye must know, per glance, what the subject is. In each zone one element dominates (the numeral, the jug, the workout name) and everything else steps back in size, weight, and contrast to support it. Two co-equal focal elements in one region force the reader to arbitrate — that hesitation is the "cluttered" feeling. Screen-level version of Canon 04 §4 #50/#51. [Gestalt figure-ground, RUI, NN/g] [judgment]
43. **Scroll position must be self-locating: any single viewport-height slice of the page must reveal which zone the user is in** — on a phone the user mostly sees one zone at a time; zone identity therefore has to be carried by things visible mid-zone (the atmosphere family, the running header style, indentation pattern), not only by the header that scrolled away. Test by screenshotting the page at several arbitrary scroll offsets. [NN/g (scanning/orientation), NS goal 2, HIG (pinned/section headers exist for this reason)] [mechanical]

## 8. One Coherent Composition

44. **One grid per screen, established in the first viewport and obeyed to the last pixel of scroll** — the invisible column/margin structure (welcomed explicitly by the north star) is decided once per surface; zones arrange differently WITHIN it (full-width, split, offset) but never bring their own margins or gutters. A zone that needs a different grid is a different page. [JMB, NS ("an invisible layout grid for alignment is expected"), Canon 04 §2 #28] [mechanical]
45. **One lighting and elevation logic per screen** — if the surface has atmosphere/depth, the implied light comes from one direction and one logic (Canon 04 §5 #64) for every zone; a glow-lit hydration scene above a flat-shadowed sleep block reads as a collage. Decide flat vs. lit once per surface. [RUI, NS goal 3, HIG] [judgment]
46. **The 30-second structure test is the acceptance gate for this whole canon** — north star goal 2 made operational: show the screen to someone (or honestly simulate it) for 30 seconds; they must be able to say how many zones there are, where each begins, and what each is about — without any zone being in a box. If they can't, return to the ladder (§1) and rhythm audit (#10) before touching styling. [NS goals 1–3, NN/g five-second-test practice extended] [judgment]
47. **Compose the page as one piece before styling any zone: block out all zones' gaps, spines, and focal points at once** — sectioning defects are compositional, so they cannot be fixed zone-by-zone; a session that polishes zones serially glues modules. Concretely: first pass places every zone's opener, beat, and boundary rung on the full-page canvas; only then does detailed work begin inside zones. Pairs with the north star's per-feature checklist (question → model → encoding → interaction → styling). [JMB (the layout precedes the elements), NS checklist, RUI] [judgment]
48. **When a zone resists cardless treatment, redesign the zone's content before reaching for a container** — the escape hatch is real but expensive; before invoking it, try: splitting the zone (it may be two zones), giving it a scene-based encoding (NS), collapsing it to a summary line with focus+context expansion, or strengthening its opener lockup. Only after those fail is a common-region container justified — and it must then be the screen's ONLY one, or the exception becomes the system. [NS escape hatch, RUI, Precedent (Stripe removed cards by *reorganizing information*, not by restyling the same boxes)] [judgment]

## Contested / no-consensus

- **Hairline dividers: legitimate tool vs. dated crutch.** Apple's HIG and iOS system lists use hairline separators pervasively; Vercel's practice and much modern editorial web design avoid divider lines almost entirely in favor of space and surface shifts, and M3's recent direction also de-emphasizes dividers. This canon takes the middle position (rung 4 of the canonical ladder, sibling-rows-only, #22), but a strict whitespace-only school would delete rung 4 and a strict iOS-native school would use it far more. Owner tiebreak welcome if sessions keep disagreeing.
- **Background-tint zones vs. "that's just a borderless card."** Some practitioners argue any tinted region is a common-region container regardless of edges, and only spacing/typography separation is truly cardless. This canon holds the full-bleed/no-radius/no-shadow line (#23–#24) keeps tint on the right side of the north star, but this is an interpretation, not consensus.
- **Exact spacing ratios.** The ≥2× between-vs-within jump (#8) is a defensible synthesis of Gestalt proximity research and RUI practice, but no source of record commits to a number; Refactoring UI argues by example, NN/g by principle. The specific beat values in #8 (8–12 / 16–24 / 56–96) are the APPROVED ds.css tokens (`--sp` scale, `--zone-gap`), not published law — the tokens are the house's number, and the tokens win.
- **Baseline grids on the web.** Classic editorial doctrine (JMB) sets all type on one baseline grid; modern web practice has largely abandoned strict baseline grids (line-height arithmetic and CSS make them brittle) in favor of consistent spacing tokens. This canon follows web practice (rhythm via the spacing beat, not baselines); a print-trained reviewer would object.
- **Per-zone atmosphere/color-family coding.** The north star's connective-tissue idea (atmosphere per zone, bleeding at seams) has strong precedent in the reference mock and in scene-based apps (Apple Weather), but thin support in the product-design literature, which mostly warns against many hues per screen (RUI, Canon 04 §3 #38). Rules #38–#39 are the reconciliation; treat them as house doctrine under owner-delegated design authority, not industry consensus.
- **Header presence on self-identifying zones (#17).** Scanning research (NN/g) favors always-labeled sections on long pages; minimalist editorial practice favors omission when form self-identifies. Left as [judgment] with a keep-when-in-doubt default.
