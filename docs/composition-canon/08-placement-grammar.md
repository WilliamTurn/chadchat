# Composition Canon 08 — Component Placement Grammar

Domain: where each kind of element lives WITHIN a group or zone — primary actions, secondary actions, metadata, helper text, status, icons, alignment. This is the positional grammar that applies inside any zone on any surface. It answers "where does this element go?", not "how does it behave?" (ux-canon 01 owns behavior), not "how heavy does it look?" (ux-canon 04 owns hierarchy weights), not form-field geometry (composition canon 03) or dialog anatomy (composition canon 04).

The failure this file fixes: sessions place same-kind elements ad hoc — helper text above one field and below another, a primary action mid-screen here and bottom-pinned there, metadata left of one title and under the next. The meta-rule of this whole file: **an element kind has exactly one home, and it lives there on every screen.**

Tag key: **[mechanical]** = checkable by rule/tool with no taste required. **[judgment]** = requires a human (or design-trained) eye, but the direction of "better" is agreed.

Sources abbreviated: HIG = Apple Human Interface Guidelines; M3 = Material Design 3 (component anatomy specs); NN/g = Nielsen Norman Group (eyetracking + button-placement research); Baymard = Baymard Institute; RUI = Refactoring UI (Wathan/Schoger); VWIG = Vercel Web Interface Guidelines; Polaris = Shopify Polaris; Carbon = IBM Carbon; ADS = Atlassian Design System; GOV.UK = GOV.UK Design System; APG = W3C ARIA Authoring Practices; WCAG = WCAG 2.1/2.2. All directional rules ("left"/"right") assume LTR locales; they mirror in RTL.

## A. Reading gravity — position itself carries meaning

1. **Start every zone at its top-left with the thing that identifies it.** Users in LTR locales begin scanning at the top-left; whatever sits there is read as "what this is." Put the zone's name or its single most important answer there — never a decoration, a control, or metadata. [NN/g eyetracking, RUI] [mechanical]
2. **Position encodes importance: top = identity and the answer, middle = evidence and detail, bottom = commitment and actions.** Height on the screen is a hierarchy signal users read without thinking. A screen or zone reads top-down as: what this is → what the facts are → what I can do about it. Placing an action above the information it acts on, or burying the answer below its supporting detail, inverts the grammar. [NN/g, HIG, Polaris ("arrange content in order of importance")] [judgment]
3. **Design against the F-pattern, don't just accept it.** Eyetracking shows users scan text-heavy screens in an F: two horizontal sweeps near the top, then a skim down the left edge. That means (a) the left edge of every list, heading, and link carries the scent — front-load the first two words — and (b) content placed right-of-center or deep in an unbroken column is effectively invisible. The F-pattern is what happens when hierarchy fails; strong headings, groups, and visual anchors break it deliberately. [NN/g F-pattern research] [judgment]
4. **Sparse screens read in a Z; put the exit action at the bottom-right.** On layouts with little text (heroes, empty states, confirmations), the eye enters top-left and exits bottom-right. Forward motion (continue, start, confirm) belongs at the bottom or bottom-trailing; backward motion (back, cancel, dismiss) belongs left or top. Right/down means "onward," left/up means "retreat" — never swap them. [NN/g, UX consensus on Z-pattern, HIG] [judgment]
5. **The first phone-height viewport carries the zone's identity and today's answer.** At ~390px wide, whatever fits before the first scroll is the screen's opening statement. It must contain who/what this screen is and its primary answer or action scent; pushing those below the fold to make room for decoration or secondary content is a placement defect. [NN/g, north-star goal 1 (5-second understanding)] [judgment]
6. **Chrome lives at the edges; content lives in the middle — and neither invades the other.** Global navigation, back, screen title, and screen-level tools occupy the top and bottom edge bars. The scrolling middle is for content and content-local actions. A nav control floating mid-content, or content crammed into a bar, breaks the user's mental model of what scrolls and what stays. [HIG (bars), M3 (app bars, navigation bar), NN/g] [mechanical]
7. **An action never sits above the thing it commits.** The user must be able to have seen everything the action applies to before reaching the control, in natural scroll order. "Delete account" above the account summary, or "Finish workout" above the remaining sets, forces the user to commit blind or scroll backwards. (Pinned bars are the audited exception — see 12.) [GOV.UK, Baymard, NN/g] [mechanical]

7a. **In RTL locales, every directional rule in this file mirrors wholesale: reading gravity enters top-right, the F/Z scan patterns flip horizontally, leading = right and trailing = left, forward motion exits bottom-left.** Mirroring is all-or-nothing per locale — a half-mirrored screen (RTL text with LTR chevrons and action order) is broken, not transitional. The spine's mirror is 06 #31a; element-level bidi mechanics (icon flipping, numerals, punctuation) are ux-canon 04 §14. Low urgency for the English-only launch, but binding on any localized build. [Material bidirectionality, HIG right-to-left, ux-canon 04 §14; added 2026-07-23 per completeness sweep THIN #5] [mechanical]

## B. Primary action — where the commit control lives

8. **The primary action of a flow lives at the bottom of that flow's content.** It is the last thing in reading order: after every field, every fact, every choice it commits. On phones this doubles as thumb reach — the bottom is the easiest tap zone. [HIG, Baymard ("last thing in the natural scanning order"), GOV.UK] [mechanical]
9. **Pin the primary action to the bottom edge only when it is valid the whole time.** A pinned (sticky) bottom button is right when the action is available throughout scrolling (start workout, add to cart, send). It is wrong when the action only becomes meaningful after the user finishes something (reading terms, filling the last field) — then it goes in-flow at the end of the content. Decide by asking: "could the user legitimately press this before scrolling?" Yes → pin; no → in-flow. [Baymard (sticky CTA research), HIG, M3 (bottom app bar)] [judgment]
10. **A pinned bottom action clears the system and never covers the last content.** Pinned bars respect safe-area insets (home indicator) and the scroll container gets bottom padding equal to the bar height, so the final line of content can always scroll fully above the bar. [HIG (safe areas), VWIG] [mechanical]
11. **Per surface archetype, pick pinned or in-flow once — then every sibling surface matches.** If workout screens pin their primary action, all workout screens pin it; if settings-style pages put it in-flow, all of them do. A user who has learned "the big button is always at the bottom edge" must never find it mid-page on the next screen. [NN/g (consistency), Polaris, house meta-rule §I] [mechanical]
12. **On desktop and tablet, a page-level primary action may also live at the top-trailing of the page header.** The enterprise-web convention: page identity (title) at the header's leading edge, page commitment (primary button) at its trailing edge. Use it only for actions that apply to the whole page (Save, Publish), never for actions scoped to one zone lower down — those live in their zone. At phone widths this header action collapses to the bottom-of-flow position, not into a cramped header. [Polaris (page header, max one primary), Carbon, ADS] [mechanical]
13. **In a horizontal action pair belonging to a dialog, overlay, or inline confirm, the primary action takes the trailing (right) position.** Continue/Confirm right, Back/Cancel left — right is forward, matching reading direction and every mainstream mobile platform. Scope, precisely: this rule covers dialog/overlay action bars (full anatomy in comp-canon 04 §28), inline confirm pairs, and editor bars. Forms and wizard steps — INCLUDING wizard footers — are not in scope: they follow forms canon 03 #40 (buttons anchored to the form's left content edge, primary first). The desktop page-header trailing primary (rule 12) is chrome, not a form pair. One convention per context, stated once; whatever a context runs, never flip it between screens. (Legacy-Windows order is the known dissent — see Contested.) [HIG, M3, NN/g ("pick one and stay consistent"); comp-canon 04 §28, forms canon 03 #40] [mechanical] *(patched 2026-07-23: wizard footers removed from scope; deferred to 03 #40)*
14. **When actions stack vertically, the primary action goes on top.** Phone-width stacks (full-width buttons) put the default/primary first and secondary beneath it — the first button the eye and thumb meet is the recommended one. Exception: when the primary action is destructive, the safe action may take the top slot so the dangerous one is not the reflex tap (ux-canon 04 §57 owns destructive de-emphasis). [HIG (alert button order), M3] [mechanical]
15. **A primary action never floats mid-screen between zones.** A big filled button sitting in the gap between two zones belongs to neither; the user cannot tell what it commits. Every action is placed inside the zone it acts on, bound to it by proximity. [RUI (proximity), NN/g] [mechanical]
16. **One home per action — don't scatter duplicates.** The same action appearing in two places in one viewport (header AND inline AND pinned) makes users wonder if they differ. The audited exception: on genuinely long pages the page-level primary may appear once at top (header) and once at bottom (end of flow) because they are never on-screen together. Two visible instances at once is the defect. [Polaris, NN/g; Baymard supports the long-page repeat] [judgment]

## C. Secondary, tertiary, and destructive actions

17. **Secondary actions sit immediately adjacent to the primary, before it in reading order.** Beside a trailing-aligned primary, the secondary sits to its left; under a full-width phone primary, it sits below. The pair reads as one decision point; a secondary action exiled to another part of the screen stops being an alternative and becomes a mystery. [M3, HIG, Polaris] [mechanical]
18. **Tertiary and rare actions retreat into an overflow menu at the zone's trailing edge.** The kebab/ellipsis lives at the top-right of the row, card-substitute, or zone header it governs — the universal learned position. Its menu orders items by frequency of use, with destructive items last and visually separated. [M3 (menus), HIG, Polaris ("unique actions top, typical object actions bottom"), NN/g] [mechanical]
19. **Destructive actions never sit where the reflex tap lands.** Keep delete/remove physically separated from confirm/continue — not the adjacent slot in a pair, not the next row in a menu without a separator, not directly under where a repeated tap (e.g. a stepper) happens. Position is your mis-tap insurance; weight de-emphasis is ux-canon 04's. [HIG, M3, NN/g] [judgment]
20. **Back and cancel live at the leading edge: top-left in chrome, left of the primary in-flow.** Retreat is always leftward/upward (rule 4). A lone cancel pinned to the bottom edge reads as the primary action of the screen — a lie of position. [HIG (back button top-left), M3, NN/g] [mechanical]
21. **Zone-scoped actions live in the zone header's trailing slot; item-scoped actions live on the item's trailing edge.** "Edit" for a whole section goes top-right of that section's heading line; "remove" for one row goes at that row's right edge. Scope is communicated by which container's trailing edge the action occupies — an item action in the zone header (or vice versa) misstates what it affects. [Polaris (card header actions), M3 (list trailing), Carbon] [mechanical]

## D. Icon-only vs labeled controls — which zone each may occupy

22. **Icon-only buttons are chrome-edge citizens; labeled buttons are content citizens.** Bars, headers, and row trailing-edges may use icon-only controls (which still need accessible names and must be universally learned symbols — ux-canon 04 §72–73 owns that). Inside the content flow, actions get visible text labels; a naked icon floating in content has no learned position to explain it. [HIG, M3, NN/g] [mechanical]
23. **The top bar has a fixed grammar: back/close leading, title leading-or-center, contextual tools trailing.** Users never search for back — it is always the leftmost thing. Trailing top-bar slots hold at most two or three icon actions before the rest collapse into an overflow menu (rule 18). [HIG (navigation bars), M3 (top app bar anatomy), Polaris] [mechanical]
24. **Global navigation sits at the bottom edge on phones, top or leading edge on desktop.** Phone thumb-reach puts the tab bar at the bottom; desktop puts primary nav in a top bar or leading sidebar. Never both patterns at once on one width, and never navigation controls scattered inside content. [HIG (tab bars), M3 (navigation bar/rail), NN/g] [mechanical]
25. **A floating action button, if used at all, owns the bottom-trailing corner — and only the screen's single most-used creation action earns it.** The FAB position is exactly one; two floating buttons, or a FAB for a secondary action, breaks the grammar. Note: the north star's cardless editorial direction rarely wants a FAB; prefer the pinned bar or in-flow primary (rules 8–9) unless the owner approves. [M3 (FAB spec); NN/g dissent noted in Contested] [judgment]

## E. Metadata grammar — where the small facts live

26. **Identity first, metadata after — never before.** In any row, header, or lockup: the name/title is the first text; timestamps, counts, statuses, authors, and categories come after it or below it in diminished style. Metadata read before identity forces the user to hold facts about a thing they haven't identified yet. [RUI (labels de-emphasized), M3, NN/g] [mechanical]
27. **The standard row anatomy is: leading identity visual → stacked title and supporting text → trailing metadata or affordance.** This is the fixed internal placement every mainstream list uses (M3 list-item spec: leading avatar/icon, headline over supporting text, trailing meta). Adopt it as the house row and never rearrange per screen — supporting text below its title, not beside; trailing slot for the smallest fact or the navigation affordance, not for a second sentence. [M3 (list anatomy), HIG (table rows), Polaris] [mechanical]
28. **Only an overline may sit above a title.** A small spaced-caps category/kicker line ("TODAY'S WORKOUT") is the one text element allowed above the title in a lockup; everything else stacks below. Two stacked small lines above a title, or metadata above the overline, muddles which line is the identity. [M3 (overline in list/card anatomy), editorial convention per north star] [mechanical]
29. **Timestamps take one position per list type, applied everywhere that list type appears.** The two legitimate homes: the row's trailing edge (top-right, message-list style) or inline after/below the title in diminished style (feed style). Pick per list archetype — conversations trailing, history feeds inline-below, say — then never mix within the app. There is no cross-source winner (see Contested); consistency is the rule. [HIG, M3, NN/g] [judgment]
30. **Counts and badges attach to the thing they count.** A numeric badge overlays the top-trailing corner of its icon; a count chip sits immediately trailing its label ("Sets · 12"). A count floating in whitespace, or on the opposite side of the row from its subject, is orphaned data. [M3 (badge spec), HIG, RUI (proximity)] [mechanical]
31. **Status has two homes: on the identity for presence, in the trailing/meta position for workflow state.** A dot overlaying the avatar's bottom-trailing corner means "state of this entity right now" (online, syncing). A status word or chip in the row's trailing slot or a table's status column means "where this item is in a process" (Completed, Skipped). Don't put workflow status on the avatar or presence dots in the meta column. [M3, Slack/messaging convention, Carbon (tag in tables)] [mechanical]
32. **Detail pages open with a title block: overline (optional) → title → one metadata line → actions; body after.** The metadata line (date, author, status, category — separated by · or spacing) sits directly under the title in diminished style. Page actions attach to this block (trailing on wide screens, below it or pinned per rule 11 on phones), and the body starts only after the block is complete. [Polaris (resource details), Carbon, editorial convention] [mechanical]
33. **In data displays, the value takes the dominant position and the label sits in one fixed slot — above or below, chosen once.** "225 lb" big with "bench press" small is right; equally weighted side-by-side pairs are not (ux-canon 04 §53 owns the weights; this rule owns the slot). If stat lockups put the label under the value in one zone, every stat lockup in the app puts it under. [RUI, dataviz consensus, house meta-rule §I] [mechanical]

## F. Helper and annotation text — where explanations may appear

34. **Explanatory text attaches directly below the thing it explains, inside that thing's group.** The gap between a note and its subject must be visibly smaller than the gap to the next element (proximity binds ownership). Form-field hint placement (between label and input) is composition canon 03's domain; everywhere else, the general rule is: annotation below the annotated. [RUI, GOV.UK, Gestalt/NN/g] [mechanical]
35. **Helper text never floats between unrelated elements.** A line of grey text sitting equidistant between two zones, or between a chart and an unrelated button, has ambiguous ownership — the user cannot tell what it qualifies. If you cannot place a note adjacent to its subject, the note or the layout is wrong. [RUI (proximity), NN/g] [mechanical]
36. **A zone-level explanation goes at the top of the zone, directly under the zone heading — once.** "Your zones update as you log workouts" belongs under the zone's title before its content, not repeated per item and not at the bottom where it's read after confusion set in. [GOV.UK (section hint), NN/g] [mechanical]
37. **When an explanation is only sometimes needed, replace inline text with an info affordance trailing its subject's label.** Persistent inline notes on every element bloat the screen (density trade-offs are composition canon 02's domain); the ⓘ/help affordance sits immediately after the label it explains — never at the far edge of the row where it detaches from its subject — and opens a popover/tooltip. Reserve it for genuinely optional depth; anything the user must know to proceed stays inline. [Baymard (CVV "what is this"), HIG (help), NN/g] [judgment]
38. **Qualifying fine print sits between the content and the action it qualifies.** "Cancel anytime" or a legal note about a commitment goes directly above (or immediately below) the commit button — the last thing read before the press. Fine print after the button, or at the top of the screen, is fine print unread at decision time. [Baymard (checkout reassurance placement), GOV.UK] [mechanical]

## G. Icon placement grammar — leading vs trailing semantics

39. **Leading icon = what this is; trailing icon = what happens.** An icon before the text identifies or categorizes (activity type, file kind, warning). An icon after the text signals the interaction's result or direction (chevron = navigates, ▾ = opens, ↗ = leaves). Swapping these — a category glyph trailing, an affordance leading — makes the row read backwards. [M3 (list/menu anatomy), HIG, NN/g] [mechanical]
40. **Trailing chevron-right means "navigates deeper"; trailing chevron-down means "expands in place" — and each is used for nothing else.** A row with chevron-right that expands, or a disclosure ▾ that navigates, breaks a promise the position+glyph made. Rows that do neither get no chevron. [HIG (disclosure indicators), M3, APG (disclosure pattern)] [mechanical]
41. **In buttons, a leading icon reinforces the verb; a trailing icon is reserved for direction or menus.** "▶ Start workout" (leading, illustrates the action) vs "Sort ▾" (trailing, opens options) vs "Continue →" (trailing, forward motion). An icon on both sides of one label is decoration, not grammar. [M3 (button anatomy), HIG, Polaris] [mechanical]
42. **Overlay positions are fixed: notification badge top-trailing on the icon, presence dot bottom-trailing on the avatar.** These two corners are learned globally; moving a badge to the bottom or a presence dot to the top reads as a rendering bug. [M3 (badge spec), messaging-platform consensus] [mechanical]
43. **External links and new-window actions declare themselves with a trailing indicator.** The ↗ (or equivalent) trails the link text — the user learns "trailing ↗ = I'm leaving this context" and must be able to trust its absence too. [VWIG, NN/g, WCAG (advance warning of context change)] [mechanical]
44. **An icon paired with text belongs to that text: same line, optically centered to it, one fixed gap token.** Icons vertically floating off their label's centerline, or gapped differently in different rows, break the lockup into two unrelated elements. (Optical alignment technique is ux-canon 04 §75; the placement rule is: the icon is inside the label's group, always the same side, always the same gap.) [RUI, VWIG] [mechanical]

44a. **Media takes one deliberate aspect ratio per component context, tokenized — never per-screen improvisation** — thumbnails square (1:1), media leads one landscape ratio chosen once (16:9 or 4:5), full-bleed scenes free-composed per the north star; the ratio is set in the shared component so every instance matches. A caption sits directly below its media, in diminished style, inside the media's group (rule 34's proximity law). An inset image may take the control radius (`--r-control`); an inset radiused block used to FRAME other content is a container and must pass 01 §4's earning tests (01 #79). Image-grid gutters use the group gap (`--sp-4`), not improvised values. [Carbon (aspect ratios), M3 (media in components), HIG imagery; 01 #79; added 2026-07-23 per completeness sweep THIN #4] [mechanical]

44b. **Reorder mode has a fixed grammar: drag handle at the row's trailing edge, drop position shown as a gap opening between rows, one explicit mode entry/exit control** — the leading edge stays with identity (rule 27), so the handle lives trailing; the dragged row lifts with the standard transient elevation (01 #36) while siblings part to show the drop gap — never a line drawn through content. Edit/reorder mode is entered and exited by one labeled control in the zone header's trailing slot (rule 21), and rows that cannot reorder show no handle (the capability must exist now — 01 #42). In a cardless list the lifted row may take a temporary surface treatment mid-drag; it returns to flat on drop. [HIG (edit mode, drag and drop), M3 (drag states); 01 #36/#42; added 2026-07-23 per completeness sweep THIN #8] [mechanical]

## H. Alignment defaults — which edge each content kind claims

45. **Text left-aligns; centering is reserved for short display content.** Body, labels, list text, and headings sit on the left edge. Center alignment is legitimate only for 1–3 line hero/display statements, empty states, and confirmation moments — and then the whole block centers on one axis (rule 49). (Ux-canon 04 §14/26 own the typographic reasons; this rule owns the default assignment.) [RUI, BPT via ux-canon 04, NN/g] [mechanical]
46. **Numbers that will be compared right-align on a shared edge.** Anywhere values stack for comparison — table columns (ux-canon 04 §12 owns tables), stat stacks, set/rep logs, leaderboards — the digits share a right edge with tabular figures so magnitude is scannable. Numbers used as labels or inline in prose stay left/inline. [BPT, NN/g, dataviz consensus] [mechanical]
47. **Every zone resolves to one strong content edge, and same-kind elements in a stack share it exactly.** All row titles on one left edge, all trailing metadata on one right edge; an element indented "almost" to the shared edge (off by a few px, or by an inconsistent leading-icon allowance) reads as broken. Reserve indentation for actual hierarchy (children indent under parents), never for variety. [RUI, NN/g, VWIG] [mechanical]
48. **Split alignment (label left, value right) is allowed only in bounded definition rows.** The settings-row pattern — label on the left edge, value/control on the right edge — works when the row is narrow enough that the eye bridges the gap (phone width, constrained max-width). Across a wide desktop container the association snaps; there, keep label and value adjacent on the left. [NN/g (settings pattern), Baymard (review tables), RUI] [judgment]
49. **A centered composition centers everything on one axis — no strays.** In an empty state or confirmation screen, the icon, heading, explanation, and action all share the center axis; one left-aligned element inside a centered block (or vice versa) reads as a defect. Mixed alignment within any single cluster is the general defect: pick the cluster's axis and commit. [RUI, M3 (empty state), NN/g] [mechanical]
50. **Inline lockups align on the text baseline, and value+unit stay unbroken.** "225 lb", "8:32 PM", icon+label pairs: baseline-aligned, non-breaking space between value and unit, never wrapped apart. A unit orphaned to the next line or a superscript-floating unit detaches the fact from its number. [BPT, VWIG (non-breaking spaces)] [mechanical]

## I. The repetition rule — placement is a system property

51. **An element kind appearing in multiple zones uses identical placement in every one of them.** Timestamps, status chips, overflow menus, info affordances, chevrons, helper text: wherever the kind recurs, its position recurs — same slot, same side, same gap. This is the consistency meta-rule made positional; every rule above defines a slot, and this rule forbids per-screen exceptions to it. [NN/g (consistency heuristic), HIG, M3, house failure statement] [mechanical]
52. **Placement lives in the shared component, not in per-screen layout code.** The row component owns where its timestamp goes; the zone-header component owns where its action goes. If a screen can move an element's position with local CSS, the grammar will drift — encode the slot once in the shared layer (CLAUDE.md §3) so drift is impossible rather than merely forbidden. [DS consensus (Polaris, Carbon, M3 anatomy specs), house rule] [mechanical]
53. **A new element kind gets one placement decision, recorded, then reused — never decided per screen.** The first screen to need, say, a sync-status indicator sets its home (per the grammar above), records it in the decision log, and every later screen inherits it. "It fit better over here on this screen" is how the ad-hoc failure starts. [DS consensus, house decision-log practice] [judgment]
54. **Never fix a local crowding problem by moving an element out of its slot.** If the timestamp doesn't fit in the trailing slot at 320px, the fix is a density/truncation decision (composition canon 02's domain) or a responsive variant applied to ALL instances of that component — not a one-screen relocation. Position is a promise; break it everywhere or nowhere. [NN/g, RUI, house meta-rule] [judgment]

## J. Interjection and chrome placement — the pinned-edge slot map *(section added 2026-07-23: completeness sweep MISSING #1 + THIN #2/#7)*

What 01 #43 decides may be contained, and ux-canon 03 scripts, this section PLACES: system messages and the sticky chrome they must coexist with. The screen-level stacking order these slots obey is 01 §5a.

55. **A page banner takes exactly one slot: directly below the top bar and above ALL content, including any filter row.** A banner is page-scoped commentary (offline, sync failure, account notice) — it must be read before the content it qualifies, so nothing content-local may sit above it. Banners tied to a persisting condition (offline) pin under the top bar until the condition clears; one-time informational banners scroll away with the content. Never below the fold, never mid-list, never stacked more than two deep — a third condition means the worst two summarize or the screen has bigger problems. Containment is 01 #43's call; wording and dismissal are ux-canon 03's. [M3 banner, Polaris banner, ADS section message; 01 #43] [mechanical]

56. **Toasts anchor bottom-center, above the topmost pinned edge — above the tab bar, above a pinned CTA, above the open keyboard — clearing `env(safe-area-inset-bottom)`, and never covering either bar's controls.** Compute the anchor from the highest pinned element's top edge, not from the viewport bottom. A toast that lands on top of the primary action it just confirmed (or under the keyboard) is the canonical placement failure. Timing, wording, and the undo affordance are ux-canon 03's. [M3 snackbar placement, HIG, VWIG safe areas; 01 §5a] [mechanical]

57. **One transient interjection at a time: a new toast replaces or queues behind the current one — never a stack — and a toast never renders over a modal scrim.** Feedback for an action taken inside a modal surface renders inside that surface, or after it closes; a toast fired "behind" an open dialog waits. When several system messages compete, the most important shows and the rest queue (severity and duration are ux-canon 03's; transient-layer caps are ux-canon 02 §17.170). [M3 (one snackbar at a time), ux-canon 02 §17.170] [mechanical]

58. **Sticky elements meet in a fixed order and displace, never overlap: at the top — bar, then pinned section header (each new one pushes the previous away), then a pinned filter row if the list earned one; at the bottom — tab bar lowest, pinned CTA above it, toast above both.** Every pinned element adds its height as scroll padding at its edge (rule 10 generalized), so no content is ever "present but underneath." The combined budget is 02 #29's 25% cap; collapse choreography runs optional-first — large title and filter row collapse on scroll-down before anything claims permanence (06 #19a). [M3 (app bar scroll behavior), HIG; 01 #20/#58a, 02 #29] [mechanical]

59. **Bulk-select mode transforms the top bar in place: leading Cancel/X, selected-count as the title, bulk actions trailing (overflowing past 2–3) — and when the actions outgrow the bar, the bulk action bar pins at the bottom in the pinned-CTA slot, above the tab bar.** Rows show selection with a leading control that shifts ALL rows' content right by one fixed allowance (never just the selected ones); entering or exiting the mode never scrolls or reflows the list. Row-level selection rendering in a cardless list is 05 #32b; bulk-action behavior is ux-canon 08 §B8. [M3 (contextual action bar), HIG (edit mode); added 2026-07-23 per completeness sweep THIN #2] [mechanical]

## Contested / no-consensus

- **Confirm-left vs confirm-right in action pairs.** macOS, iOS, Material, and the modern web put the affirmative action trailing (right); legacy Windows put OK first (left). NN/g's own position is that neither order is a usability catastrophe and consistency matters more than the choice. This canon adopts trailing-primary (rule 13) as the house rule because every mobile platform the owner's users know does it; the genuinely settled part is "never mix orders."
- **Form submit alignment: left edge vs trailing edge.** GOV.UK left-aligns the submit button under the fields' left edge (keeps it in the F-scan path); Material and most enterprise systems trail-align it. Both are defensible; composition canon 03 (form geometry) should fix the house choice — this file only requires that the choice be singular and app-wide.
- **Pinned/sticky primary CTA vs in-flow.** Baymard's commerce research supports sticky bottom CTAs (measurable conversion lift); HIG and NN/g caution that persistent bars cover content and can invite premature commits. Rule 9's validity test ("could the user legitimately press this before scrolling?") is this canon's synthesis, not a cross-source verbatim.
- **Repeating the primary action on long pages.** Baymard supports a top+bottom repeat for long product pages; Polaris and NN/g lean one-home-per-action. Rule 16 allows the repeat only when the two instances can never be co-visible; treat that as house synthesis.
- **The FAB.** M3 actively specifies and promotes it; NN/g and HIG-culture critique it (covers content, low discoverability of what it does, no label). Given the north star's editorial cardless direction, this canon treats the FAB as needing owner approval (rule 25).
- **Timestamp position in lists.** Messaging apps trail it top-right; feeds and news apps put it inline below the title; no source of record declares one correct. Rule 29 demands per-archetype consistency instead of a universal slot.
- **Status chip vs status dot vs status text.** Systems disagree on how much visual weight workflow status gets and whether it lives in a dedicated column, the trailing slot, or under the title. Rule 31's two-home split (presence-on-identity vs workflow-in-meta) is the widest common denominator, not a spec citation.
- **Info affordance (ⓘ) vs always-visible helper text.** GOV.UK is hostile to hiding needed help behind disclosure; Baymard endorses contextual "what is this" affordances for occasional-need info. Rule 37's "must-know stays inline" boundary is the reconciliation; the borderline cases stay judgment calls.
