# Composition Canon 03 — Form and Input Layout Geometry

Domain: the visual assembly of anything a user fills in — where labels sit, how wide fields are, when fields may share a row, how unit labels attach, where help/placeholder/error text lives, how forms group into sections, and the one-input-system consistency rule. Input BEHAVIOR (validation timing, keyboards, autofill, commit models, control choice) is already law in `audits/workout-overhaul-2026-07-22/ux-canon/01-interaction-and-input.md` (cited below as "ux-canon 01 #n"); this file owns GEOMETRY only.
Sources: Luke Wroblewski, *Web Form Design* (LW — still the canonical text; eyetracking basis noted at end), Baymard Institute (largest empirical body; checkout/form field studies), Nielsen Norman Group (NN/g), GOV.UK Design System (most rigorously tested public form system; component pages re-verified current July 2026), Apple Human Interface Guidelines (HIG), Material Design 3 text-field specs (M3), Shopify Polaris, IBM Carbon, WCAG 2.2, Refactoring UI (RUI).
Tags: [mechanical] = a lint/test could check it. [judgment] = needs human review.

## A. Label geometry — where the label sits

1. **Top-aligned labels are the default: label above its field, both sharing the same left edge.** Eyetracking shows top-aligned labels are read and acted on in a single fixation — the eye never has to jump sideways — and they are the only placement that survives every width from 320px to desktop without restacking logic. Baymard's mobile checkout studies reach the same verdict independently. — [LW, Baymard, GOV.UK, M3, NN/g] — [mechanical]
2. **Left-of-field (side) labels are a conditional exception, never a house default: only for dense, desktop-only editing surfaces where users scan many labels to find one field — and they must restack to top-aligned below tablet width.** Side labels slow first-time completion (eye ping-pongs between two columns) but speed re-scanning of long known forms; that trade only exists on wide screens. If a surface is ever used on a phone, this option is off the table. — [LW, NN/g] — [judgment]
3. **If side labels are ever used, right-align the label text toward its field.** LW's eyetracking found right-aligned side labels roughly halve completion time versus left-aligned ones because the label ends where the field begins; the cost is a ragged left edge, which is why top-aligned still wins overall. — [LW] — [mechanical]
4. **Never use the inside of the field as the label's home.** Placeholder-as-label is banned by ux-canon 01 #27 (behavior); the geometric corollary is that no layout may depend on text inside the box to identify the field — the visible label always occupies its own space outside the input border. Floating labels (M3's label-slides-up pattern) are [contested] — see end. — [NN/g, GOV.UK, Baymard, WCAG 3.3.2] — [mechanical]
5. **A label sits closer to its own field than to anything else on the screen.** Concretely: the label-to-own-field gap must be at most half the gap from one field block to the next (e.g. 4–8px label→field, 20–32px field→next label). When the gaps are near-equal, the eye cannot tell which field a label belongs to — the single most common cause of "our forms look off." — [LW, RUI, NN/g (Gestalt proximity), M3 spacing] — [mechanical]
6. **Keep labels to one short line; if the label needs a second sentence, that sentence is help text, not label.** Top-aligned labels earn their keep by being scannable; a three-line label destroys the rhythm and pushes the field away from its name. — [GOV.UK, NN/g] — [judgment]
7. **Checkboxes and radios reverse the geometry: control on the left, label to its right, top-aligned to the label's first line of text.** This is the one control family where the "label above" rule does not apply; when the label wraps, the control stays aligned with line one, never vertically centered against the whole paragraph. — [GOV.UK, M3, HIG] — [mechanical]
8. **The label (and its help text) is part of the field's click/tap target.** Geometrically the label block and input form one interactive unit with no dead gap between them; programmatic association is ux-canon 01 #28, but the visual gap must also be small enough that they read as one unit. — [WCAG, GOV.UK] — [mechanical]

## B. Field width as affordance — width signals the answer

9. **Field width is information: it must signal the expected length of the answer.** A user reads a short box as "short answer expected" before reading the label. Baymard found mismatched widths cause measurable hesitation — users pause, re-read the label, and second-guess what is being asked. — [Baymard, GOV.UK, LW] — [judgment]
10. **Known-length answers get fixed widths sized in characters, not percentages.** A 2-digit minutes field is ~2–4ch plus padding; a 4-digit year is ~4–5ch; a postcode is postcode-sized. GOV.UK ships explicit 2/3/4/5/10/20-character width classes for exactly this. — [GOV.UK, Baymard] — [mechanical]
11. **Unknown-length answers get a comfortable capped width, never full-bleed on wide screens.** Name, email, and free-text fields grow to a sensible max (roughly 320–480px for single-line text) and stop; a form field spanning a 1400px viewport is a defect. — [GOV.UK fluid widths, RUI, NN/g] — [mechanical]
12. **The uniform-full-width-everything form is a defect on desktop.** When every field is the same width, width stops carrying meaning and short-answer fields (age, reps, minutes) actively mislead. Exception: on phones, fields with genuinely variable answers may all run full-column width — the narrow column restores the affordance — but fixed short fields stay short even there. — [Baymard, GOV.UK] — [judgment]
13. **No field may ever exceed its container's content box.** `max-width: 100%` (with border-box sizing) on every input, at every width, inside every dialog. GOV.UK's own rule: fixed-width inputs stay fixed "unless wider than the viewport, in which case they shrink to fit." A field that forces a horizontal scrollbar inside a dialog — the duration-field failure this canon exists for — is a hard defect. — [GOV.UK, ux-canon 04 #30 (no horizontal scroll)] — [mechanical]
14. **Same expected answer, same width, everywhere.** A "minutes" field on the workout screen and a "minutes" field in a dialog are the same width; per-screen improvisation is how "our fields always look inconsistent" happens. Width classes belong in the design system (ds.css tokens), not in page CSS. — [GOV.UK width classes, Carbon, Polaris] — [mechanical]
15. **Short fields still need breathing room: wide enough for their content, the caret, and any browser-injected icon.** GOV.UK's fixed widths deliberately include extra padding because browsers inject reveal/autofill icons; a 2-digit field cut to exactly two glyph widths clips its own content. — [GOV.UK] — [mechanical]

## C. Multi-field rows — when fields may share a line, and how units attach

16. **One field per row is the default; a row may be shared only when the fields form one answer in the user's head.** Day/month/year, minutes/seconds, feet/inches, hours/minutes — one mental quantity split across boxes. Two unrelated questions side by side is the multi-column error (ux-canon 01 #17) wearing a disguise. — [Baymard, GOV.UK, NN/g] — [mechanical]
17. **A shared row is a labeled group: one group label for the whole answer, plus a small label on each part.** GOV.UK's date input is the reference geometry: legend "Date of birth" above, then three boxes each with its own small "Day / Month / Year" label. A row of naked boxes with only positional meaning fails the moment a screen reader or a distracted user hits it. — [GOV.UK, ux-canon 01 #18 (fieldset/legend)] — [mechanical]
18. **Design the shared row at 320px first: the sum of fixed field widths + gaps + unit labels must fit the narrowest container the row will ever live in.** Do the arithmetic — if a min/s pair with unit labels cannot fit a 320px dialog's content box, the row is over-designed (fields too wide, gaps too big), because the fields themselves are only 2–4 characters. The row never clips and never scrolls. — [GOV.UK, responsive consensus; house failure case 2026-07] — [mechanical]
19. **Unit labels (min, s, kg, lb, %) attach to their field as a prefix/suffix inside the field's connected construct — never as loose text floating in the row.** Use the design system's input-affix slot (GOV.UK prefix/suffix component, M3 suffix text, Polaris connected/prefix-suffix): the unit lives inside or flush against the field's border, in a non-wrapping inline unit with it. A unit that can wrap to its own line, orphan, or clip against an edge (the "Duration [0] min [0] s" failure) is structurally wrong, not just mis-styled. — [GOV.UK, M3, Polaris] — [mechanical]
20. **Prefix/suffix text matches the field's height, sits vertically centered to the input text, and must not look pressable.** An affix styled like a button invites dead taps; an affix taller or shorter than its field reads as broken. — [GOV.UK, M3] — [mechanical]
21. **In a shared row, the input boxes align on one horizontal axis even when their labels differ in length.** Align the boxes (align-items to the input edge), not the label tops; a row where one wrapped label pushes its box lower than its siblings reads as a bug. — [Carbon, Polaris, RUI (alignment)] — [mechanical]
22. **Within-row gaps are smaller than between-row gaps.** The min/s pair sits tight (8–12px between parts); the next question sits clearly further away. Same proximity law as labels: spacing encodes which things are one answer. — [RUI, ux-canon 04 #23] — [mechanical]
23. **Short-field rows do not restack at phone widths; only rows of wide fields do.** A min/s or day/month/year row fits 320px by design (rule 18) and stacking it makes it worse — the parts stop reading as one quantity. Pairs like first/last name that cannot fit stack instead. This is the primary rule for fixed short fields; the wrap fallback in comp-canon 04 §46 applies only to rows containing *variable-width* fields — the two rules split the territory, not contradict. — [GOV.UK date input, Baymard mobile; comp-canon 04 §46] — [judgment] *(patched 2026-07-23: boundary with 04 §46's wrap fallback stated)*
24. **Date entry geometry: three separate boxes sized to content (2ch/2ch/4ch), visibly one group.** Which mechanism (typed vs picker) is behavior law (ux-canon 01 #52–53); the geometry when typed fields are used is fixed short boxes in a labeled row, not three full-width stacked fields. — [GOV.UK, Baymard] — [mechanical]
25. **A field plus its stepper/adornment (+/− buttons, reveal toggle, clear button) is one visual unit with a single outer boundary.** Adornments sit inside or flush to the field's border at full field height; a stepper floating 12px away from its number is two components pretending to be one. — [HIG, M3, Polaris] — [mechanical]

## D. Help text, placeholder, and error geometry — fixed slots, no jumping

(What help/error text says and when it appears is ux-canon 01 #30–31, #41–51. This section owns only where it lives.)

26. **Every field has fixed slots: label, then help text, then input, then error/counter zone — same order on every field in the app.** GOV.UK's order (label → hint → input, error between hint and input) and M3's order (label → input → supporting-text line below) are both defensible; what is not defensible is mixing them. Pick one slot map system-wide and token-ize it. House note: ux-canon 01 #31 already adopted hint-between-label-and-input (the GOV.UK slot), so that is the standing default. — [GOV.UK, M3, Carbon] — [mechanical]
27. **Help text that changes what the user is about to type sits before the input (below the label); afterthought text (counters, "saved" notes) sits after.** Format requirements shown below the field are routinely discovered only after the mistake — and on phones the software keyboard covers the below-field zone while typing. — [GOV.UK, Baymard] — [mechanical]
28. **Error messages appear in one fixed slot adjacent to the offending field — the same slot for every field, every screen.** Inline-adjacent is behavior law (ux-canon 01 #43); the geometry rule is the slot's position never varies: if errors render above the input on one screen and below it on another, users must re-learn where to look each time. — [GOV.UK, M3, NN/g] — [mechanical]
29. **When error or help text appears, the layout may push down; it must never shift anything horizontally, overlay a neighboring field, or move the focused field out from under the user's caret.** Two accepted strategies: reserve the text line permanently (M3's recommendation — zero shift), or let content push down vertically (GOV.UK — accepted because the push happens on submit/blur, not mid-keystroke). Choosing between them is [contested] — see end; choose once, apply everywhere. — [M3, GOV.UK, Vercel WIG (CLS)] — [mechanical]
30. **Error and help text wrap within the field's own column width; they never widen the row or spill under a neighboring field.** A long error under the minutes field must not push the seconds field or run beneath it — the text belongs to one field and stays in its lane (or, for grouped rows, attaches to the group per rule 17's geometry). — [GOV.UK, Carbon] — [mechanical]
31. **Error state marks the field itself as well as the text: border/accent change on the input box, message with an icon, colors from the semantic tokens.** The geometric point: the marked element is the input box the user must fix, so the eye lands on it from anywhere on screen; text-only errors get lost. (Color-plus-icon redundancy is ux-canon 01 #46.) — [GOV.UK (red border), M3, WCAG 1.4.1] — [mechanical]
32. **Placeholder text renders in the input's own size but visibly lighter than a filled value — and light enough that a prefilled value can never be mistaken for a placeholder.** The two states must be distinguishable at a glance, or users skip fields that are actually empty (or retype ones that are filled). Whether placeholders should exist at all beyond format examples is behavior law (ux-canon 01 #30). — [NN/g, Baymard, M3] — [mechanical]
33. **Character counters live in the after-input slot, and only on fields with real limits.** One position (commonly below, aligned to one side — pick per system), never inside the field where they collide with the value. — [GOV.UK character count, M3, Polaris] — [mechanical]
34. **A help icon/popover attaches to the label line, not floating inside or beside the input box.** Inside the box it collides with the value and reveal/clear icons; on the label it reads as "about this question." — [Baymard (CVV help), Polaris, Carbon] — [mechanical]

## E. Form section structure — grouping, headings, pacing

35. **One-column form layout, restated as geometry: the fill-in path is a single straight line down one left edge.** Documented exceptions, all from section C: short related parts of one answer on a row (dates, durations, ranges), and tightly-paired short fields the user reads as one item. Nothing else earns a second column, at any width. — [Baymard, GOV.UK, NN/g, ux-canon 01 #17] — [mechanical]
36. **Group related fields with whitespace first; add a section heading only when a group is big enough to name.** NN/g's white-space studies show spacing alone communicates grouping; headings are for genuinely separate topics (Profile / Goals / Notifications), not every 2-field cluster. Cardless north star applies: grouping comes from proximity and headings, not boxes around each group. — [NN/g, LW, design-north-star] — [judgment]
37. **Three-tier vertical rhythm, from tokens: gap within a row < gap between field blocks < gap between sections.** One value per tier, applied identically on every form; a section heading sits closer to the fields below it than to the section above it (heading belongs to what follows). — [RUI, M3 spacing, ux-canon 04 #33] — [mechanical]
38. **The whole form is a narrow column with a max-width (~400–600px for typical single-column forms) regardless of viewport.** On desktop the column left-aligns with the page's content edge per the surface's layout grid; it does not center-stretch to fill. This is the canonical desktop form-column placement: centering is legitimate only when the whole screen is a centered composition (onboarding, celebration — comp-canon 02 #44 draws exactly this line); 05 #55 and 07 #18 defer here. — [GOV.UK (two-thirds column), RUI, NN/g; comp-canon 02 #44] — [mechanical] *(patched 2026-07-23: declared canonical; centered-composition exception scoped)*
39. **Radio/checkbox groups get the same label geometry as fields: group label styled like a field label, options indented to one consistent edge below it.** The group label-to-options gap follows rule 5 (closer to its own options than to the previous block). — [GOV.UK, M3] — [mechanical]
40. **Action buttons anchor to the form's left content edge (LTR), primary first, and this placement never varies between forms.** GOV.UK left-aligns submit under the last field at the same edge the eye has been tracking all along. This rule owns ALL forms and wizard steps, including wizard footers. Dialog and overlay action pairs follow the trailing convention instead (comp-canon 04 §28; ux-canon 01 #10) — the rule is each context has exactly one convention, and 08 #13 is scoped to the overlay pairs only. On phones, full-width buttons are acceptable; on desktop, buttons size to their label. — [GOV.UK, LW ("path to completion"), M3 dialogs; comp-canon 04 §28, 08 #13] — [mechanical] *(patched 2026-07-23: wizard footers claimed here; 08 #13 re-scoped)*
41. **Pace long forms by sections that each fit roughly one screen; when a form outgrows a few screens, split into steps rather than growing the scroll.** Step mechanics are behavior law (ux-canon 01 #62–66); the geometric trigger is here: a single form column longer than ~3 phone screens is due for splitting or pruning. — [GOV.UK, Baymard] — [judgment]
42. **Progressive disclosure geometry: conditionally revealed fields appear directly below their trigger, indented or connected to it, and expansion pushes content down without shifting anything else sideways.** The revealed field must read as belonging to the answer that summoned it. — [GOV.UK conditional reveal, ux-canon 01 #67] — [mechanical]

## F. Required and optional marking

43. **Mark the minority, in text, appended to the label: "(optional)" or "(required)" in lighter weight on the same line.** If most fields are required, mark only the optional ones. Marking-the-minority is behavior law (ux-canon 01 #19); the geometry law is the marker's home: inside the label line, immediately after the label text — never right-floated across the column, never inside the field, never a bare color change. — [GOV.UK, Baymard, NN/g] — [mechanical]
44. **If an asterisk convention is used instead, the asterisk sits immediately after the label text with a one-line key at the top of the form — and the choice (asterisk vs "(optional)" text) is made once for the whole app.** Mixed conventions across screens are exactly the inconsistency the owner reports. GOV.UK's own current lean is to avoid asterisks entirely; see contested. — [Baymard, GOV.UK] — [mechanical]

## G. One input system — the consistency law

45. **The app has exactly one text-input component, and every field is an instance of it: same height, same padding, same border weight, same corner radius, same label/help/error slot map, same focus treatment.** Variation between fields may encode only two things: width (rule 9) and type-specific affordances (suffix unit, reveal toggle, stepper). Everything else identical, sourced from ds.css tokens — a field styled in page CSS is a defect even if it looks fine. (DS gap, flagged 2026-07-23: ds.css today ships only `--r-control`; the input component's height, affix-slot, and focus-ring tokens are on the chadchat port session's list — until they land, this rule is the spec they must satisfy.) — [M3, Polaris, Carbon, GOV.UK; house ds.css rule] — [mechanical] *(patched 2026-07-23: DS token gap flagged for the port session)*
46. **All controls that can share a row share an exact height: text input, select, date parts, and any adjacent button align to one control-height token.** M3's reference is 56dp fields (dense variants exist but pick one density per surface); the specific value matters less than there being exactly one. A 44px input beside a 40px button is the classic tell of hand-rolled controls. — [M3 specs, Carbon, Polaris, ux-canon 04 #34] — [mechanical]
47. **Selects, comboboxes, and date/picker fields wear the same box as the text input — same border, height, radius, label geometry — differing only by their affordance icon (chevron, calendar).** A dropdown that looks like a different species from the text field above it breaks the form's single visual voice. — [M3, Polaris, Carbon] — [mechanical]
48. **One focus treatment everywhere: same ring/border color, width, and offset on every focusable field, meeting 3:1 contrast against adjacent colors.** Focus is the state users see most while filling a form; two focus styles on one screen reads as two products. — [WCAG 2.4.11/2.4.13, M3 state layers, ux-canon 04 #44] — [mechanical]
49. **Every field meets touch-target floors: input hit area ≥ 44–48px tall on touch surfaces (WCAG minimum 24px is a floor, not a target), including short numeric fields.** A 2ch-wide minutes field is narrow, not short — its height and tap area match every other field. — [HIG (44pt), M3 (48dp), WCAG 2.5.8] — [mechanical]
50. **Input value text is the body size, ≥16px on mobile (iOS zoom rule), and identical across all fields.** Cross-ref ux-canon 04 #4; geometry point: value text, label text, and help text each have exactly one size/weight token app-wide. — [Vercel WIG, HIG, ux-canon 04] — [mechanical]
51. **The full state inventory — rest, hover, focus, filled, error, disabled — renders identically on every field.** If the error border is 2px red on one screen it is 2px red on all of them; state styling belongs to the shared component, never the page. — [M3, Polaris, Carbon, ux-canon 01 #1] — [mechanical]
52. **Dialogs, sheets, and inline surfaces use the same input system — no "dialog-sized" variant.** Compact one-off inputs invented inside dialogs are where the duration-field failure came from; a dialog is just a narrower container (rules 13 and 18 handle the width). — [Polaris, Carbon; house failure case] — [mechanical]

## Contested / no-consensus

- **Floating labels (M3's label-animates-into-the-border pattern).** Material uses them as the default; GOV.UK, NN/g, and Baymard all recommend against (small start size, ambiguous filled state, animation cost, poor fit with prefix/suffix). Given the house default is a static top label (rule 1), floating labels should not be adopted without an owner decision; the sources genuinely disagree.
- **Help text above vs below the field.** GOV.UK (hint above, below label) vs M3/Polaris/Carbon (supporting text below). Both are internally consistent systems; there is no cross-source winner. ux-canon 01 #31 already picked the GOV.UK slot for this app — treat that as settled house law, but know it is a choice, not a consensus.
- **Reserve-space vs push-down for error/supporting text — RESOLVED BY OWNER (2026-07-23, decision log): RESERVE SPACE, system-wide.** "Nothing ever jumps": layouts hold a fixed slot for error/supporting text rather than pushing content down at error time. M3 leans this way; GOV.UK pushes down — the house pick is reserve. Rule 29's pick-one requirement is satisfied; sessions build reserve-space everywhere.
- **Which field pairs may share a row.** Universal agreement on date parts and unit pairs (min/s); genuine disagreement on first/last name (Baymard tolerates on desktop, GOV.UK stacks everything), city/postcode, and expiry/CVV (Baymard shows them rowed in checkout patterns, GOV.UK-style systems stack). Default stack when unsure.
- **Required-field asterisks vs "(optional)" text.** Baymard's data supports explicit marking of both or the minority; GOV.UK currently avoids asterisks and prefers making everything required with "(optional)" exceptions; enterprise systems (Carbon) still document asterisks. One convention app-wide is the only firm rule.
- **Placeholder contrast.** WCAG does not formally require 4.5:1 for placeholder text (it's "temporary" content in most readings), but low-contrast placeholders fail real users; sources split on whether placeholders should simply be dropped instead of styled.
- **Uniform full-width fields on phones.** Baymard endorses full-column-width fields on mobile (top labels exist partly to enable this); the width-as-affordance camp still shortens known-length fields there. This canon's resolution (rule 12: variable-length may fill, fixed-length stays short even on phones) is a synthesis, not a sourced consensus.

## Sourcing-honesty notes

- **Luke Wroblewski's *Web Form Design* is from 2008.** Its eyetracking studies (with Etre) were small-N; the top-aligned-label and right-aligned-side-label findings have been replicated directionally by Baymard and NN/g since, which is why they stand, but the precise completion-time multipliers often quoted from it should not be treated as current measurements. No successor text has displaced it as of mid-2026.
- **Baymard's full research is paywalled.** Claims here rest on their public articles (mobile label-position study, field-width hesitation findings, checkout patterns) verified via search July 2026, plus their widely republished conclusions. Specific participant counts and percentages were not independently re-verified.
- **GOV.UK component pages (text input, date input, error message, character count) were confirmed live and current July 2026** via web search; the fixed-width classes, shrink-to-viewport behavior, hint placement, and date-input group geometry cited are from those current pages.
- **M3 dp values (56dp field height, 8dp paddings) were verified against the current m3.material.io text-field specs page** via search July 2026; M3 continues to evolve (expressive updates through 2025–26), so treat exact dp numbers as M3's, not universal law.
- **Apple HIG is thin on form-layout geometry** (it defers heavily to platform controls); it is cited here mainly for touch-target and adornment-unity rules, not label/width geometry.
- **Polaris and Carbon citations are from training knowledge, not re-fetched this session.** Both systems are stable and their form patterns (connected affixes, single input component, state inventories) are long-standing, but exact current wording was not re-verified.
- **Rules 18 and 52 encode a house failure case** (the clipping duration field, screenshot-verified 2026-07), not an external source; they are consistent with GOV.UK's shrink-to-fit rule and Vercel WIG's no-horizontal-scroll rule but the 320px-first arithmetic framing is ours.
