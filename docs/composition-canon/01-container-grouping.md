# Composition Canon 01 — Container and Grouping Logic

Domain: how a screen is assembled from groups — what makes adjacent content read as one group, how neighboring groups get separated, and the exact conditions under which a group earns a visible container (card, box, panel, bordered or tinted region) instead of sitting directly on the surface. This is the layer between the design tokens (`app/design-system/ds.css`) and aesthetic taste: the decision rules a session applies before styling anything.

Relationship to the north star: `design-north-star-2026-07-16.md` sets the direction — cardless by default at all widths, grouping via Gestalt principles, containers as a justified last resort. This file makes that direction operational. Both observed failure modes (boxing everything, then over-correcting to "cards banned everywhere") come from the absence of a decision rule, not from picking the wrong absolute. Accordingly, every rule below is conditional — a test you run — never a blanket ban or a blanket permission.

Tag key: **[mechanical]** = checkable by rule/tool with no taste required. **[judgment]** = requires a design-trained eye, but the direction of "better" is agreed.

Sources abbreviated: RUI = Refactoring UI (Wathan/Schoger); M3 = Material Design 3 (including its containment and canonical-layouts foundations articles and the cards/divider component guidelines); HIG = Apple Human Interface Guidelines (Boxes, Layout, Materials); NN/g = Nielsen Norman Group (Gestalt series incl. "The Principle of Common Region," "Cards: UI-Component Definition," whitespace articles); VWIG = Vercel Web Interface Guidelines; Polaris = Shopify Polaris (card layout pattern, spatial organization); Carbon = IBM Carbon (tile usage); ADS = Atlassian Design System; Baymard = Baymard Institute; GOV.UK = GOV.UK Design System; Palmer 1992 = Stephen Palmer, "Common region: A new principle of perceptual grouping," *Cognitive Psychology* (the original common-region research); NS = the project north star document. "DS consensus" = agreement across mature design systems. Cross-references of the form "ux-canon 04 #23" point at `audits/workout-overhaul-2026-07-22/ux-canon/`.

## 1. How groups form without containers (the Gestalt baseline)

1. **Exhaust invisible grouping before drawing anything — in order: proximity, alignment, similarity, continuity** — the eye groups content by closeness, shared edges, matching appearance, and flowing arrangement long before it needs a drawn boundary. A session that reaches for a box first has skipped four free tools that don't cost pixels or add chrome. [NN/g Gestalt series, RUI, NS] [judgment]
2. **When two things must read as one group, move them closer before you enclose them** — proximity is the default grouping mechanism; the spacing mechanics (within-group spacing visibly smaller than between-group spacing, one fixed spacing scale) are ux-canon 04 #21–23 and are not restated here. The composition rule this file adds: if a grouping problem can be solved by changing distances alone, solving it any louder way is over-design. [RUI, NN/g proximity; ux-canon 04 §2] [judgment]
3. **Use shared edges to declare membership and indents to declare subordination** — items that start on the same left edge read as siblings in one family; shifting a sub-group's edge inward reads as "belongs to the thing above." An alignment change is a real separator (rung 2 of the ladder in §2) and often removes the need for any drawn line. [RUI, NN/g; ux-canon 04 #24] [judgment]
4. **Style identically what is the same kind of thing; never vary styling decoratively** — similarity is a grouping force: two lists styled the same read as the same type of content even far apart, and one item styled differently reads as a different type. Every styling difference is therefore a *claim* about a difference in meaning, and unfounded claims mislead. [NN/g similarity, RUI, DS consensus] [judgment]
5. **Arrange series content on one continuous line or column so it reads as a series** — continuity makes a row of day markers or a column of history entries read as one sequence with no chrome at all; breaking the line (staggering, wrapping mid-thought) breaks the perceived series. [NN/g continuation, Gestalt literature] [judgment]
6. **Treat common region (an enclosing boundary or shared background) as the strongest grouping force — and ration it accordingly** — Palmer's experiments and NN/g's demonstrations show a drawn boundary overrides proximity and similarity: boxed items group together even when they sit closer to things outside the box. That power is exactly why containers are rationed: a wrongly drawn box lies louder than wrong spacing ever can, and a correct box spent on a trivial grouping leaves nothing stronger for when it's needed. [Palmer 1992, NN/g common region, NS] [judgment]
7. **Use literal connecting lines only for genuinely connective content (steppers, timelines, diagrams)** — connectedness (a drawn line between elements) is even stronger than common region, but in product UI it reads as flowchart; reserve it for content whose meaning IS the connection, like progress steps or a workout-history timeline. [NN/g connectedness, Gestalt literature] [judgment]
8. **Audit what your whitespace accidentally claims** — whitespace is a grouping material, not leftover margin: an uneven gap reads as a group boundary whether you meant it or not. When a screen "feels disorganized" without any container being wrong, the usual cause is spacing that claims groupings the content doesn't have. [RUI, NN/g whitespace] [judgment]
9. **Remember the page surface is itself a common region** — everything sitting directly on the background reads as belonging to one coherent surface, which is the north star's "continuous information surface." This is why flat composition works at all: the screen already provides the container; groups drawn inside it are subdivisions, and each one fragments the whole. [NN/g, NS] [judgment]
10. **Verify grouping with a squint/blur pass** — blur the screenshot (squint, or apply a blur filter): the blobs that remain are the groups users actually perceive. If the blurred blobs don't match the intended groups, fix grouping before styling; if they do match without any borders present, borders would be redundant. [RUI, practitioner consensus (see sourcing note)] [mechanical]

## 2. The separation ladder

11. **Know the ladder: every boundary between adjacent groups uses one of these rungs, ordered weakest to strongest** — (0) shared flow, no boundary; (1) a whitespace step; (2) an alignment shift or indent; (3) a section header / overline label; (4) a hairline divider; (5) a background-tint zone; (6) a hairline-bordered container; (7) an elevated (shadowed) card; (8) a separate surface (sheet, dialog, page). Higher rungs are louder, cost more pixels, and make stronger claims. Naming the rung turns "should this be a card?" into "which rung does this boundary need?" — a question with a checkable answer. This is THE canonical separation ladder for the whole composition canon: 05 and 06 reference these rung numbers and define no ladder of their own; each rung is a *substitute* for the ones below at that boundary, never an addition to them (#14). The zone-level atmosphere/color-family shift (06 #38–39) is the named variant of rung 5 — a full-bleed tint whose hue carries zone identity. [Synthesized from RUI (space → background color → border ordering), NN/g common region ("can I communicate this grouping by simply adding or removing whitespace?"), M3 containment (implicit → explicit), HIG; see sourcing note] [mechanical] *(patched 2026-07-23: declared canonical; 06's parallel ladder merged in)*
12. **Use the weakest rung that makes the boundary unambiguous to a first-time viewer** — this is the single decision rule of this file. Start at whitespace; escalate only when a rung demonstrably fails (a viewer could misread which items belong together). RUI's ordering is explicit: to separate elements, "you don't always need a border — try more spacing, or a different background color, first." [RUI, NN/g common region, M3 divider guidance ("consider using space instead"), NS] [judgment]
13. **Escalate one rung at a time, with a reason per step** — jumping from "whitespace felt weak" straight to an elevated card skips five cheaper fixes. Each escalation names what was ambiguous at the rung below. This is what prevents the reflexive card. [RUI, NS; practitioner consensus (see sourcing note)] [judgment]
14. **One boundary marker per boundary; combine layers only when each answers a different question** — a header + divider + tint + border around the same group says the same thing four times. Legitimate combinations pair a *name* with a *separator* (a section header above, hairline dividers between the items inside — the HIG grouped-list pattern); illegitimate ones stack multiple separators on the same edge. Border-plus-strong-shadow doubling is ux-canon 04 #65. [RUI, HIG, M3; ux-canon 04 #65] [judgment]
15. **When torn between two rungs, take the lower — under-separation is cheap to fix, over-separation is contagious** — adding space later is a one-line change; but once one group gets boxed, its flat neighbors suddenly look unfinished and get boxed "for consistency," and the surface tiles over. The historical card-grid dashboards this project rejected grew exactly this way. [RUI, NS; practitioner consensus (see sourcing note)] [judgment]
16. **Separators carry information only when they are scarce — N groups need at most N−1 boundaries** — if every gap has a divider, the dividers say nothing and become texture; if only the topic changes have them, each one means something. Before adding a separator, count the ones already on screen. [RUI ("if everything is emphasized, nothing is"), Tufte data-ink logic, M3 divider ("use dividers sparingly")] [mechanical]
17. **Pick the rung by semantic distance, not by content volume** — sub-steps of one task get low rungs no matter how tall they are; a change of topic gets a header; a change of mode, ownership, or provenance gets more. A big group is not automatically an important boundary, and a one-line group of genuinely foreign content (an error banner) can outrank it. [NN/g, HIG, M3 containment] [judgment]
18. **Containers separate AND claim objecthood — never use one when you only need separation** — rungs 0–5 just divide; rungs 6+ additionally announce "this is a discrete thing you can act on, move, or remove as a unit" (that is what edges + corners + radius read as). Users try to tap cards. If the group is not an actionable unit, a container over-promises; use rung ≤5. [NN/g cards (card as single clickable unit), M3 cards, Carbon tile (base/clickable/selectable/expandable), Baymard hit areas] [judgment]
19. **Rung 8 (a separate surface) is for content that cannot share the screen's context — never smuggle a mini-dialog into the page as a card** — if a group needs its own header, its own actions, and isolation from the page flow, it wants a sheet, dialog, or page (choosing which is ux-canon 02's overlay-selection territory), not a heavy card pretending to be one. Conversely, content that CAN share context should not be exiled to an overlay to avoid a layout problem. [HIG, M3, NN/g; ux-canon 02] [judgment]
20. **Boundaries that must survive scrolling use sticky headers with on-scroll shadow, not permanently heavier chrome** — when a section label pins under the app bar, a shadow or hairline appearing only-while-scrolled marks the boundary without taxing the resting layout. Don't promote a section to a card just so its edge stays visible mid-scroll. [M3 top-app-bar/scroll behavior, HIG, VWIG] [mechanical]

## 3. Rules per rung

### Whitespace (rung 1)

21. **Whitespace alone separates reliably when groups are few and internally tight** — with roughly 2–6 groups whose internal spacing is visibly tighter than the gaps between them, space is fully sufficient and is the north star default. When a group's *internal* spacing is loose (sparse content, big visuals), the between-group gap loses contrast and stops reading as a boundary — that, specifically, is the signal to consider the next rung, not a general feeling that space is "too plain." [RUI, NN/g whitespace/common region, NS] [judgment]
22. **Between-group space must sit at least two steps above within-group space on the spacing scale** — e.g. `--sp-2` (8px) inside a group, `--sp-4`/`--sp-5` (16–24px) between groups, per the canonical gap table (06 #8). One step (8 vs 12) is imperceptible as a boundary; the two-scale-step form is the implementation of the ≥2× floor the table mandates. The scale itself is ux-canon 04 #21; this rule fixes the *ratio* that makes rung 1 work. [RUI, Gestalt/NN/g; ux-canon 04 #21, #23; 06 #8] [mechanical] *(patched 2026-07-23: values re-anchored to ds.css tokens via 06 #8)*

### Alignment shift (rung 2)

23. **Use an indent or edge change when groups are parts of one continuing flow that whitespace alone leaves ambiguous** — sub-items indent under their parent; a hanging label column separates labels from values; a switch from centered hero to left-aligned body marks "intro is over." This keeps everything on one surface while still drawing a readable boundary. [RUI, NN/g; ux-canon 04 #24] [judgment]

### Section header (rung 3)

24. **Add a section header when the group needs a NAME — when users will look for it by name or its membership isn't self-evident; omit it when the content is self-labeling** — "Sleep" over a sleep visualization a user must find deserves the overline; a header reading "Stats" over three obviously-stat numbers is noise (dropping needless labels is ux-canon 04 #54). Header style comes from the ds.css spaced small-caps overline tokens, not ad-hoc bold text. [NN/g, RUI, HIG boxes ("provide a succinct introductory title if it helps clarify"), NS typography] [judgment]
25. **A titled, whitespace-separated group does nearly everything a titled card does — reach for this substitution first** — HIG's own alternative to boxes is "padding and alignment to communicate additional grouping"; GOV.UK builds entire government services on header + spacing with almost no boxes. When a session's instinct says "this section needs a card," the tested substitution is: overline header, tightened internal spacing, bigger gap to neighbors. [HIG boxes, GOV.UK, RUI, NS] [judgment]

### Divider (rung 4)

26. **Use a hairline divider where space can't be afforded or the eye must cross the boundary repeatedly — after considering space first** — M3's divider guidance is literal: dividers group content in lists and containers, "consider using space" before adding one. Long scanning lists, dense menus, and tables are where dividers beat whitespace (space tall enough to separate would bloat the list). One-off boundaries between page sections usually want rung 1–3 instead. [M3 divider guidelines, RUI, NN/g] [judgment]
27. **Dividers are hairline, low-contrast, and tokenized** — 1px (or 0.5px on high-density displays), a quiet grey that is visible in both themes but never competes with content; the color comes from a token, not a per-screen hex. A divider you notice as an object is too strong. [M3, RUI, VWIG; ux-canon 04 §5] [mechanical]
28. **Full-width vs inset dividers carry different meanings — use them accordingly, never decoratively, and only WITHIN one surface** — full-width dividers close a group inside its surface (or mark expandable areas, per #26's space-first framing); inset dividers separate related siblings that remain one group (list items sharing a left icon column). Mixing the two at random inside one list destroys the distinction. Between two unlike ZONES a divider is the wrong tool at any width — that seam belongs to the whitespace beat and header grammar (06 #22, the house position; dividers are the dense-context substitute per 02 #37). [M3 divider guidelines; 06 #22] [mechanical] *(patched 2026-07-23: full-width dividers scoped to within-surface closure per 06 #22)*
29. **Skip dividers between fewer than ~5 well-spaced siblings** — short groups with adequate spacing don't need per-item lines; the dividers just add stripes. As the list grows, or density tightens (see #68), dividers start paying for themselves. [RUI, NN/g, M3 ("use dividers sparingly")] [judgment]
30. **Never let a full-width divider sit where it can read as the end of the page** — a strong horizontal line near the fold creates a "false floor": users stop scrolling because the line says "done." Prefer inset dividers or spacing near likely fold positions, and let content visibly peek past the fold. [NN/g common region + false-floor research] [judgment]

### Background-tint zone (rung 5)

31. **Use a tint zone when a region must read as different in KIND but not as an object** — a contextual atmosphere (the north star's hydration-blue and sleep-purple zones), a page area distinguishing chrome from content, a "current" row highlighted in a list. The shared background groups its contents (common region) without edges promising tappability. Text on the tint must still meet contrast (ux-canon 04 #41) and survive both themes. [NN/g common region ("placing items on a shared background makes them more likely to be perceived as related"), M3 surface roles, NS] [judgment]
32. **Bleed tint zones to at least one screen edge; a floating tint rectangle with visible corners is a borderless card and counts as rung 6** — full-bleed tint reads as a region of the environment (the north star's "connective tissue," atmospheres bleeding into each other); an inset rounded tint block reads as an object and takes on all of a container's obligations (#18, §4). Decide which one you mean. [NS; practitioner consensus (see sourcing note)] [judgment]
33. **One tint level between the page surface and content — don't stack tints** — a tinted group inside a tinted zone forces a third contrast step and reads as nesting (§5 applies). M3's surface-container ramp offers five steps, but spending more than two on one screen area turns the layout muddy; pick the one boundary that matters. [M3 surface container roles, RUI; practitioner consensus (see sourcing note)] [mechanical]
34. **Zebra striping is a tint-zone technique for wide, dense tables — not for narrow lists** — alternating row tints help the eye track across many columns; in a phone-width list they add stripes where a divider or spacing would do. (Row treatment details belong to the tables canon, ux-canon 08.) [NN/g common region (zebra stripes example); contested at the margins — see Contested] [judgment]

### Bordered container (rung 6) and elevated card (rung 7)

35. **When a border is earned (§4), it is hairline and its internal padding exceeds its visual weight** — a heavy stroke or cramped padding chokes the content and reads as a form field or a warning; the container should be quieter than what it contains. Border tokens and the border-as-last-resort principle live in ux-canon 04 #66 and §5; this file adds the earning tests that gate reaching rung 6 at all. [RUI, VWIG (semi-transparent hairlines); ux-canon 04 #65–67] [mechanical]
36. **Reserve elevation (shadow) for containment that is interactive as a unit or floats above the flow — static informational sections never earn a shadow** — elevation means "closer to the user, more temporary or more actionable" (ux-canon 04 #62): menus, draggable items mid-drag, a card that is one big button. A shadowed box around passive stats lies about the z-order. If a group earns containment but is not interactive, stop at rung 6 (or 5). [M3 elevation, HIG, RUI; ux-canon 04 #61–62] [mechanical]
37. **A container's radius comes from the token scale, and everything inside obeys concentric-radius math** — ux-canon 04 #67 owns the arithmetic (inner radius = outer radius − padding); VWIG states the same (child radius ≤ parent, concentric). This file adds: needing radius arithmetic at all is a smell to re-check depth (§5). [VWIG, HIG concentricity; ux-canon 04 #67] [mechanical]

## 4. When a group earns a container

38. **A group earns rung 6+ containment only by passing at least one earning test — and "it's a section of content" is not one of them** — the tests are: single-target (#39), collection-member (#40), provenance (#41), object-lifecycle (#42), interjection (#43), input-region (#44). Each is a real, checkable property of the content. Topical grouping of the app's own static content — the most common reason sessions draw cards — is served by rungs 1–5. [Synthesized from NN/g cards, M3 cards, HIG boxes, Carbon tile, RUI, NS — see sourcing note; each component test is individually sourced below] [mechanical]
39. **Single-target test: if the whole group is one tap/click target acting as a unit, containment communicates the hit area** — a summary block that navigates to its detail page, a selectable plan option, a workout tile that opens the workout: the edge tells users "everything in here is the button" (and on mobile, distinct hit areas measurably reduce mis-taps). Corollary: if only a fraction of the group is interactive, do NOT contain the whole group — the edge would over-promise (see Contested #2 for cards with nested actions). [NN/g cards ("clicking or tapping anywhere on the card"), Baymard mobile hit areas, Carbon clickable tile] [mechanical]
40. **Collection-member test: repeating items that users select, compare, or act on individually may each earn an identical container — but homogeneous, scan-only items prefer flat rows** — a grid of workout templates each with its own menu earns per-item containment (each is an object with identity). A history feed users merely scan down is better as divider-separated rows: NN/g's research is direct — cards suit *browsing heterogeneous* content and de-emphasize ranking; lists beat cards for scanning, searching, and comparing. Ask: browse or scan? act-on-item or read-down? [NN/g cards vs lists, M3 cards ("a card collection"), Carbon, Baymard] [judgment]
41. **Provenance test: content not authored in the app's own voice gets a marked boundary** — user-generated content, quoted messages, third-party embeds, promotional/sponsored units, and code/log excerpts are contained so users can see exactly where foreign content begins and ends (for UGC images specifically see ux-canon 04 #81; ad-labeling is also a trust/regulatory matter). The app quoting *itself* (its own stats, its own copy) has uniform provenance and earns nothing by this test. [ux-canon 04 #81, NN/g, platform ad-policy consensus, DS consensus] [mechanical]
42. **Object-lifecycle test: anything users dismiss, drag, reorder, or delete as a unit needs edges** — you cannot visually pick up or swipe away "some text and whitespace"; the container is what makes the object graspable and its removal legible (the gap it leaves closes). M3 explicitly frames dismissible/draggable behavior as card behavior. A static group that merely *could* someday be reorderable does not pass — the capability must exist now. Reorder-mode composition (handle placement, drop indicators, mode entry/exit) is 08 #44b. [M3 cards (swipe-to-dismiss, drag), HIG drag and drop, NN/g; 08 #44b] [mechanical] *(patched 2026-07-23: cross-ref to new drag-mode grammar)*
43. **Interjection test: system messages injected into a flow get contained so they read as interruptions, not content** — inline error/warning/info banners, undo bars, and update prompts are foreign to the surface's narrative; a tint or hairline boundary (usually rung 5–6 with a semantic color) marks "the system is speaking." This is why an alert banner may legitimately be the only boxed thing on an otherwise flat screen — it genuinely differs in kind. [ADS section message, Polaris banner, M3 snackbar/banner, HIG] [mechanical]
44. **Input-region test: free-form input areas need a visible bounded region so users know where "inside" is** — a multi-line composer, drop zone, signature pad, or drawing canvas earns an edge because the boundary IS functional information (where can I type/drop?). Single form fields and field groups are the forms canon's territory (see #46 and ux-canon 01); this test covers composed input surfaces. [HIG text views, M3 text fields rationale, DS consensus] [mechanical]
45. **Overlay surfaces (dialogs, sheets, popovers, menus) are already containers — content inside them starts flat, at rung 0** — the overlay spent the containment budget by existing; groups within it separate by whitespace, headers, and dividers. A box inside a dialog must independently pass an earning test (#38) — this is precisely the "Mark all unchecked sets" failure: a dialog option is the dialog's own content (same provenance, not dismissible, not a collection member), so it sits directly on the dialog surface exactly like its sibling "Workout notes." [HIG (nested boxes "busy and constrained"), M3 dialogs, NN/g; NS] [mechanical]
46. **Forms are structured by labels, proximity, and section headers — not per-field or per-fieldset cards — unless a field group passes object-lifecycle** — GOV.UK and Baymard build flat single-column forms; boxing each field group adds chrome that competes with the fields themselves. The exception falls out of the tests, not taste: a repeating, removable group (a "Set 2 — reps/weight" block the user can delete or reorder) passes #42 and earns its edges. [GOV.UK, Baymard form research, NN/g; ux-canon 01 owns field mechanics] [judgment]
47. **Contain the minimal unit that passes the test** — if the dismissible banner earns a box, the banner gets the box — not the section that happens to hold it. If each collection item earns containment, the items get contained — not the collection wrapper too (the collection groups by similarity and rhythm, #4). Widening containment beyond the passing unit is how single justified cards metastasize into boxed screens. [RUI, NS; practitioner consensus (see sourcing note)] [mechanical]
48. **Passing several tests raises confidence, not chrome — the strongest single need picks the treatment** — a draggable, dismissible, tappable card still gets one hairline-or-shadow treatment per the elevation rules (#36), never border + shadow + tint stacked as trophies. [ux-canon 04 #65; M3] [mechanical]
49. **A container approaching the size of its parent separates nothing — unbox it** — HIG's boxes rule: keep a box "relatively small in comparison with its containing view"; as it nears the container's size it stops communicating grouping and starts crowding. A card spanning the whole content column (common on phones — see #63) or a "wrapper" box around an entire screen is the page wearing a costume; remove it and let the page surface do its job (#9). [HIG boxes, RUI, NS] [mechanical]
50. **A lone boxed group on an otherwise flat screen must name its earning test or lose the box** — one boxed thing among flat siblings is a loud claim of difference in kind (#4, #18). An interjection banner passes (#43). "This section felt important" does not — importance is expressed through the hierarchy tools (scale, weight, position, color; ux-canon 04 §4), not through containment. [NN/g, RUI, NS] [judgment]
51. **A deliberately excerpted preview earns single-target containment; the same data displayed in place does not** — "today's workout" as a tappable summary navigating to the full workout passes #39. The same numbers rendered as the page's own content (no navigation, nothing to act on) are just content — compose them into the surface. The test is what the user can DO, never what the data IS. [NN/g cards, Carbon clickable tile, NS] [judgment]

## 5. Nesting depth

52. **Count containment depth from the page surface (depth 0); every common region increments it — cards, bordered groups, inset tint blocks, dialogs, sheets, expanded panels** — full-bleed tint zones (#32) and headers/dividers do not increment (they separate without enclosing). Counting is what makes the depth rules below checkable rather than vibes. [Derived counting convention — see sourcing note; HIG, M3] [mechanical]
53. **Depth 1 is the working maximum for static content; depth 2 requires each level to independently pass an earning test** — legitimate depth-2 cases exist and are always "an object inside an object": attachment chips inside a message composer (#42 inside #44), a quoted message inside a chat bubble (#41 inside #41), selectable option tiles inside a dialog (#39 inside rung 8). If either level can't name its test, flatten that level. [HIG boxes ("adding nested boxes to define subgroups can make your interface feel busy and constrained"), M3 canonical layouts, ux-canon 04 #36] [mechanical]
54. **Depth 3 is never the answer — restructure instead** — by the third boundary, padding rent (each level pays twice its padding in width), concentric-radius arithmetic, and surface-contrast steps are simultaneously exhausted; no styling rescues it. The three restructures, in order of preference: flatten the middle container to a header or tint (most common fix); promote the innermost content to its own surface (sheet/page, rung 8); merge two levels into one. [HIG, RUI, ux-canon 04 #36; NS] [mechanical]
55. **Inside a container, create subgroups with padding and alignment, not more boxes** — this is HIG's literal guidance for boxes and the standing answer to "how do I organize the inside of my one earned card": rungs 1–4 all still work inside a container; the inner ladder restarts at whitespace just as the outer one did. [HIG boxes, RUI, Polaris (adjust padding in nested contexts)] [mechanical]
56. **Inner boundaries step DOWN in strength from their parent's** — when depth 2 is earned, the inner object uses a quieter treatment than the outer (hairline or tint inside a bordered card; reduced radius per Polaris's "reduce the border radius of inset surfaces" and the concentricity rule, ux-canon 04 #67). An inner boundary as strong as its parent's makes the levels unreadable. [Polaris spatial organization, VWIG nested radii, M3] [mechanical]
57. **Elevation never nests — a shadowed card inside a shadowed card implies two contradictory light models** — shadows communicate height above the page (ux-canon 04 #62); stacking them inside each other breaks the physical metaphor. Earned inner containment uses border or tint, never a second shadow; the only shadows above a card belong to transient layers (menus, drag states). [M3 elevation, HIG, RUI; ux-canon 04 #61–62] [mechanical]
58. **A table or list inside a card is only defensible when the card holds OTHER content too; a container whose sole content is one table/list is the table wearing a frame** — Polaris states this directly (nest tables within cards only "when accompanied by other content"). Give the bare collection its own flat presentation with a header instead. [Polaris spatial organization] [mechanical]

## 5a. Z-order: the screen-level stacking contract *(section added 2026-07-23: completeness sweep MISSING #2)*

58a. **Every screen obeys one stacking order, bottom to top: page content → sticky section headers → pinned bottom action bar → bottom navigation → FAB (if owner-approved, 08 #25) → toasts/snackbars → non-modal sheets → scrim → modal sheets and dialogs → alerts spawned by an open dialog** — this is the contract that prevents things being "present but underneath" (the motivating screenshot class). A layer may only visually sit on layers below it in this list; if a design seems to need an inversion (content over a pinned bar, a banner over a dialog), the element is in the wrong layer, not the order in the wrong file. Transient-layer count limits are ux-canon 02 §17.170. [M3 elevation/z-order semantics, HIG modality, ux-canon 02 §17.170, ux-canon 04 §5] [mechanical]

58b. **One scrim per screen, owned by the topmost modal surface** — stacked modal layers (a dialog over a sheet) never sum scrims into a darker pile; the single scrim logically re-parents beneath the topmost modal, and closing that layer returns it to the one below. Two visible scrim densities at once reads as a rendering bug. Scrim opacity/fade values are ux-canon 04 §5. [M3 scrim, HIG, ux-canon 04 §5] [mechanical]

58c. **No layer hides what the current task needs: every pinned or floating element reserves its own footprint** — pinned bars add their height as scroll padding so the last line of content clears them (08 #10); toasts anchor above the topmost pinned edge (08 #56); a FAB or floating element must not permanently cover list content with no scroll escape. Feedback for an action taken inside a modal surface renders inside that surface (or after it closes), never as a toast trapped under its scrim. [HIG safe areas, M3 snackbar, VWIG; 08 §J] [mechanical]

## 6. Viewport: how the thresholds move between 320px and desktop

59. **The earning tests never change with viewport — but the PRICE of a rung does, so the rung chosen may** — a container costs at minimum twice its internal padding plus its margins in horizontal pixels: at 384px, a 16px-padded card inside the `--gutter` page margin (24px at phone widths) spends 80 of 384 pixels (~21%) on chrome before any content renders; at 1280px the same chrome is a small fraction and buys visible structure. An earned container pays that rent knowingly — it is the ONE extra inset the horizontal budget permits (02 #13, the single arithmetic). Content that borderline-earns rung 6 on desktop should drop to full-bleed rows + dividers (rung 4) on phones. This is "padding is rent, paid by function." [HIG boxes size rule applied to small screens, M3 window size classes, Baymard mobile; 02 #13; arithmetic is derived — see sourcing note] [mechanical] *(patched 2026-07-23: arithmetic recomputed from the --gutter token)*
60. **Rendering the same semantic at different rungs per breakpoint is correct responsive design, not inconsistency — but the mapping must be one rule, not per-screen improvisation** — collection items may be containers in a desktop multi-column grid and divider-separated full-bleed rows in the phone's single column (M3's canonical layouts adapt exactly this way; every mainstream mail/news app does it). The transform (e.g. "collection items: cards ≥ 840px, rows below") is decided once in the shared component, so every collection in the app dissolves identically. [M3 canonical layouts/window size classes, NN/g cards vs lists, DS consensus; CLAUDE.md §3] [mechanical]
61. **When containment dissolves on phones, the hit area and per-item identity must survive** — the flattened row keeps the whole-row tap target with a visible pressed state, keeps its per-item actions at full touch size, and keeps a readable boundary (divider or spacing) between items — Baymard's mobile research shows indistinct hit areas in flattened lists cause mis-taps. Dissolving the card is a restyle, never a demotion of function. [Baymard mobile hit areas, M3 state layers; touch sizes are ux-canon 05's] [mechanical]
62. **Side-by-side content raises the separation requirement; stacked content lowers it** — on desktop, unrelated regions sitting horizontally adjacent may need one rung more (a pane boundary: divider or tint between columns) than the same content stacked on mobile, where a vertical whitespace step suffices — horizontal gaps read as column gutters, not boundaries, so whitespace alone is weaker between columns than between stacked sections. [M3 panes/canonical layouts, HIG (iPadOS/macOS split views), NN/g] [judgment]
63. **The surface's chosen density moves the divider threshold, not the container threshold** — compact, data-dense surfaces (per ux-canon 04 #34) earn dividers earlier because their whitespace budget is spent; but density never upgrades a boundary to rung 6+ — containment is earned by the §4 tests only, at every density. [M3 density, Carbon, RUI; ux-canon 04 #34] [judgment]
64. **Never introduce a container to patch a responsive defect** — boxing text "so it doesn't feel wide" is solved by measure/max-width (ux-canon 04 #5, #26); boxing content "to stop it colliding" is solved by the grid and spacing; boxing a section "so it holds together on desktop" is solved by alignment (#3). Containers acquired this way fail every earning test and then propagate (#15). [RUI, VWIG; ux-canon 04 §2] [mechanical]
65. **When one separation mechanism must survive every width, prefer tint zones and whitespace over boxes** — full-bleed tint costs no horizontal pixels at 320px and scales expansively at desktop; a bordered container is expensive precisely where the owner's users live (~384px phones). This is why the north star's zones are atmospheres, not tiles. [NS, M3 surface roles; practitioner consensus (see sourcing note)] [judgment]

## 7. Consistency: peers, vocabulary, and screen-level coherence

66. **The peer rule: two groups of the same kind on one screen sit on the same rung — always** — the canonical failure this canon exists to prevent: "Mark all unchecked sets" boxed in its own nested card while sibling "Workout notes" sat flat on the same dialog surface. Both were dialog-native options — same provenance, same interactivity class — so both belong flat (#45). Run the check mechanically: list the screen's groups, classify each by kind (which earning tests it passes, or none), and confirm same-kind groups share a rung. [NN/g similarity + consistency heuristic, DS consensus, NS] [mechanical]
67. **Different treatment between siblings must map to a difference the user can discover** — when one sibling genuinely differs (one banner is dismissible, its neighbor is permanent), the visual difference must express *that* difference (the dismissible one has edges and a close affordance) — and a first-time viewer asked "why does this one look different?" should be able to guess. If the honest answer is "different sessions built them," it is a defect. [NN/g, RUI; NS] [judgment]
68. **Keep one containment vocabulary per product: each rung means one thing everywhere** — if full-bleed tint means "contextual zone" on Home, tint cannot mean "disabled area" on Settings; if hairline containers mean "tappable summary," a decorative hairline box elsewhere teaches users a false affordance. Record the rung-to-meaning mapping beside the tokens (ds.css) so sessions inherit it instead of re-deriving it. (DS gap, flagged 2026-07-23: this mapping does not exist in ds.css yet — it is on the chadchat port session's list.) [DS consensus (M3 shape/elevation semantics, HIG consistency), NN/g; mapping-table practice is practitioner consensus — see sourcing note] [mechanical] *(patched 2026-07-23: DS gap flagged for the port session)*
69. **Collection members are uniform in structure and containment; emphasis inside a collection uses hierarchy, not a different containment species** — a "featured" or "today" item may escalate via scale, color, or position (ux-canon 04 §4), but a mix of boxed and unboxed members reads as two different content types (#4). If one member truly IS a different type (an interjection or promo inside a feed — #41, #43), its different containment is earned and must also be labeled as what it is. [NN/g similarity, M3 cards collections ("cards in a collection are cohesive"), Baymard] [mechanical]
70. **Containment decisions live in shared components, not per-screen markup** — the rung for "history row," "summary tile," or "inline alert" is decided once where the component is defined; screens compose components and inherit their containment. Per-screen hand-rolled boxes are how peer violations enter (and are already banned by CLAUDE.md §3 / design-lint). [DS consensus, CLAUDE.md §3] [mechanical]
71. **Same content type on sibling screens = same rung** — the category-history list looks the same on every category; the settings groups on two settings screens separate identically. A user who learns one screen has learned them all; that is what "one coherent visual model" (north-star goal 3) cashes out to at the composition layer. [NN/g consistency, HIG, NS goals] [mechanical]
72. **A screen that seems to need many different separator strengths at once is usually a screen doing too many jobs — check the IA before adding chrome** — needing headers + tints + boxes + dividers simultaneously to keep content apart is a symptom of overloaded scope; splitting the screen or re-sequencing the flow (ux-canon 02's territory) beats escalating the whole ladder at once. [NN/g, M3 canonical layouts; ux-canon 02] [judgment]
73. **States keep their group's rung: a section does not grow a box when empty, loading, or erroring** — empty states render as guidance in the same flat position (ux-canon 04 #85, #136); skeletons mirror the final containment exactly (ux-canon 04 #110); an error *within* a section adds an interjection (#43) inside the section, not a box around it. State changes that mutate containment make the layout feel unstable. [NN/g, M3, ux-canon 03/04] [mechanical]

## 8. The escape hatch, operationalized

74. **Every new group starts uncontained (rung ≤ 4); moving up requires answers to three questions — and the answers get written down** — (1) Which rung below failed, and what exactly was ambiguous there? (#12–13) (2) Which §4 earning test passes? (#38) (3) Is this the minimal unit (#47) at a legal depth (§5)? A container added with all three answered is correct and needs no apology; a container missing any answer is the defect the north star names. Record the passing test in the closing report so the reviewer can audit the claim. [NS escape hatch, RUI, NN/g; procedure is house-operational — see sourcing note] [mechanical]
75. **"Genuinely no other way" (the north star's clause) is satisfied exactly when #74 is satisfied — cite the test, not the vibe** — the owner's last-resort language is not a mood to be argued about; it is the state where an earning test passes and the lower rungs demonstrably failed. This converts the escape hatch from a negotiation into a checklist, which is what makes it usable by a session that never read the owner's history. [NS, house-operational] [mechanical]
76. **"Cards are banned" is the mirror-image failure and equally wrong — the tests legitimately REQUIRE containment when they pass** — dismissible banners without edges can't be seen as dismissible (#42–43); indistinct hit areas cause mis-taps (#39); unmarked UGC blurs authorship (#41); flattening a genuinely per-item-actionable collection produces the disorganization the owner also rejects. A session refusing an earned container must justify that refusal against the failing test, same burden as adding one. [NS ("borders are not banned outright"), NN/g, Baymard, M3] [mechanical]
77. **The subtraction test before shipping: hide every container's border/background in devtools — anything that stays unambiguous didn't need its box** — this is NN/g's own question ("can I communicate this grouping by simply adding or removing whitespace?") turned into a 30-second mechanical pass. Run it on the changed surface at 384px and desktop; delete what fails to justify itself, then re-run the squint pass (#10). [NN/g common region, RUI fewer-borders; procedure operationalized — see sourcing note] [mechanical]
78. **The addition test in review: for every visible boundary, name the two things it separates and the rung's justification in one sentence** — "hairline between history and recommendations because whitespace collapsed at 320px" passes; "the card makes it look finished" fails. A boundary nobody can justify in a sentence is decoration, and decoration drawn as structure misleads (#4, #18). [RUI, NN/g; procedure operationalized — see sourcing note] [mechanical]
79. **Container-shaped decoration counts as containment — pills, blobs, outlined chips, and heavy rounded outlines used for grouping are common regions and obey every rule here** — the north star's anti-goal is explicit: swapping cards for circles/pills/blobs is "same disease, different shapes." A row of outlined pills grouping filter options is at rung 6 and needs the same earning test as a card (interactive chips pass #39/#40; decorative pills fail). Likewise an inset, radiused image or media block used to FRAME other content is a container and takes the same tests; an image that merely depicts content may take the control radius freely (media placement grammar: 08 #44a). [NS anti-goals, Palmer 1992 (any closed boundary forms a region); 08 #44a] [mechanical] *(patched 2026-07-23: inset rounded media explicitly covered)*
80. **When genuinely torn between tint (5) and border (6) after all tests, take tint on phones and either on desktop — and surface the recurring case to the owner** — tint is cheaper at small widths (#59, #65) and claims less (#18); if the same borderline case recurs across sessions, it needs a recorded house decision (decision log per CLAUDE.md §8), not repeated re-litigating. [NS ("avoid as much as practically possible, on every device"), house-operational] [judgment]

## Contested / no-consensus

Sources genuinely disagree on the following. Sessions must not silently pick a side; borderline cases go to the decision log (CLAUDE.md §8).

1. **How freely dividers may be used.** Polaris is near-prohibitionist ("divider lines are reserved for data and index tables"); M3 and HIG use dividers broadly in lists, menus, and grouped tables (with "consider space first"); RUI/NN/g sit in between (whitespace-first, dividers as fallback). This canon's #26–29 takes the M3/RUI middle, but the Polaris position shows respected systems draw the line much tighter. [contested]
2. **Cards that are both a single target AND contain their own buttons/menus.** NN/g leans toward the card as one clickable unit; M3 explicitly supports cards with multiple internal actions plus a clickable surface (via state layers); Baymard documents that mixed hit areas inside one container cause mis-taps on mobile. No consensus on whether a whole-card target may coexist with nested actions; #39's corollary takes the cautious reading. [contested]
3. **Cards vs flat lists for homogeneous feeds — RESOLVED BY OWNER (2026-07-23, decision log).** Feeds of identical repeating items render CARDLESS — flat rows separated by typography, spacing, context, background, or thin lines per the ladder; on mobile this is emphatic (card padding double-taxes a width-starved screen). A feed item earns a card only by passing the §4 tests, which the owner expects to be rare. This resolves the CLAUDE.md §5 (mainstream mechanisms) vs north-star tension in the north star's favor for feeds: the mainstream card-feed pattern is explicitly declined. Sessions treat flat as settled law, not an interim default. [resolved — owner decision 2026-07-23]
4. **Bordered groups inside large desktop dialogs/windows.** Apple's own desktop software (System Settings) uses grouped boxes inside windows, and M3's canonical layouts place cards inside panes; mobile-first guidance (and #45) keeps overlay content flat. Whether a large desktop surface (window, wide dialog, pane) counts as "already a container" or as a fresh page surface is genuinely unsettled — depth arithmetic (#52) differs by a level depending on the answer. [contested]
5. **Ladder order of tint (5) vs border (6).** This canon ranks tint below border because a border + radius claims objecthood (#18) while full-bleed tint does not. But by raw perceptual strength, a solid background region can group more powerfully than a hairline (it survives squinting; NN/g's shared-background examples). The ordering here is a house ordering justified by the objecthood claim, not a universal fact. [contested — house ordering]
6. **Zebra striping.** NN/g presents it as a legitimate common-region technique for tracking across wide tables; minimalist table guidance (Tufte-derived, some enterprise systems) treats it as noise below a width/column threshold. #34's "wide tables yes, narrow lists no" is the majority view, not unanimous. [contested at the margins]
7. **Form sections in containers.** GOV.UK and Baymard ship flat single-column forms; Polaris and Carbon wrap form sections in cards as their canonical admin-app layout. This is a real philosophical split between government/consumer simplicity and enterprise-admin conventions; #46 follows the flat camp because the north star commits this product to it. [contested — house-resolved toward flat]
