# UX Canon 06 — Performance, Mobile-Platform Craft, and Trust

Domain: the app feeling fast, native, and trustworthy. Part of a six-file canon; see Cross-references at the end for principles owned by sibling files.

Tags: [mechanical] = objectively checkable/automatable; [judgment] = requires human evaluation. [contested] items are quarantined at the bottom.

---

## A. Core Web Vitals and load-speed budgets

1. **Hit LCP under 2.5 seconds for 75% of real users.** Largest Contentful Paint is when the main content (hero image, headline) appears; past 2.5s users perceive the page as slow and start abandoning. Measure on real field data (CrUX), not just your dev machine. — [web.dev/lcp] — [mechanical]
2. **Hit INP under 200 milliseconds for 75% of real users.** Interaction to Next Paint measures how long after any tap/click/keypress the screen visibly responds; over 200ms the app feels laggy or broken. INP replaced FID in 2024 and is the most commonly failed vital. — [web.dev/inp] — [mechanical]
3. **Keep CLS under 0.1.** Cumulative Layout Shift measures content jumping around while loading; shifts cause misclicks and feel broken. Nothing the user is reading or about to tap may move. — [web.dev/cls] — [mechanical]
4. **Judge performance at the 75th percentile on mid-range mobile, not on your dev machine.** Your laptop on office wifi is 5-10x faster than the median user's phone on cellular; budgets that pass only on fast hardware are failed budgets. Test throttled (e.g., 4x CPU slowdown, Slow 4G). — [web.dev/vitals, Google CrUX methodology] — [mechanical]
5. **Keep Time to First Byte under ~800ms.** Server response time is the floor under everything else; a slow TTFB makes every other optimization irrelevant. Use CDNs, edge rendering, and caching. — [web.dev/ttfb] — [mechanical]
6. **The above-the-fold content loads first; everything else waits.** Prioritize the LCP element (preload its image, no lazy-loading on it, server-render it); defer analytics, chat widgets, and below-fold content. Third-party scripts never block first paint. — [web.dev, Vercel Web Interface Guidelines] — [mechanical]
7. **Set an explicit performance budget and enforce it in CI.** Pick numbers (JS kB, image kB, LCP ms) and fail the build when exceeded; without an enforced budget, performance only ever degrades. — [web.dev/performance-budgets, Lighthouse CI] — [mechanical]
8. **Never lose user-perceivable speed to a metric you aren't watching.** Field-monitor vitals continuously (RUM), not just at launch; regressions arrive silently with every dependency bump and feature. — [web.dev/vitals-field-measurement] — [mechanical]
9. **Reserve space for everything that loads late.** Images, ads, embeds, and banners get explicit width/height or aspect-ratio boxes so arriving content never pushes the page down. This is the single biggest CLS cause. — [web.dev/cls, web.dev/optimize-cls] — [mechanical]
10. **Never insert content above what the user is reading.** Late-arriving banners, "app install" bars, and notifications must overlay or push from the bottom, never shove existing content down. — [web.dev/cls] — [mechanical]
11. **Break up long main-thread tasks; nothing blocks for more than ~50ms.** Long JavaScript tasks are what make taps feel dead (INP). Chunk work with `scheduler.yield()`/`requestIdleCallback`, move heavy computation to Web Workers. — [web.dev/optimize-long-tasks] — [mechanical]
12. **First screen renders without waiting for client-side JavaScript.** Server-render or statically render the initial HTML; a blank screen that waits for a JS bundle to boot is the canonical slow-app failure. — [web.dev/rendering-on-the-web, Next.js docs] — [mechanical]

## B. Perceived performance and responsiveness

13. **Acknowledge every input within 100ms, even if the work takes longer.** The 0.1s threshold is where interaction stops feeling instant. If the operation is slow, respond instantly with a pressed state, spinner, or optimistic change — the acknowledgment is the UX, not the completion. — [Nielsen response-time limits, NN/g] — [mechanical]
14. **Anything over ~1 second gets a progress indicator; anything over ~10 seconds gets a progress bar with escape.** 1s is where flow breaks; 10s is where attention is lost entirely — long operations need percent-done, time estimates, and a cancel. — [NN/g response times] — [mechanical]
15. **Use optimistic UI for actions that almost always succeed.** Likes, toggles, adds, renames: update the screen immediately, sync in the background, and roll back with an error (or Undo) on the rare failure. Waiting on a round-trip for a toggle reads as broken. — [Vercel WIG, NN/g] — [judgment]
16. **Delay spinners ~150-300ms and keep them visible a minimum ~300-500ms.** A spinner that flashes for 50ms on a fast response is worse than nothing — it makes fast feel janky. Show indicators only when the wait is real, and don't let them flicker. — [Vercel Web Interface Guidelines] — [mechanical]
17. **Use skeleton screens that match the real layout, not generic spinners, for content loads.** Skeletons set expectations of structure and shrink perceived wait — but only if their shape matches what arrives, so nothing jumps on swap. (Tag harmonized with canon 03 §2.11 on 2026-07-22: a lint/test can check show-delay, min-visible duration, and layout match, so [mechanical]; the where-to-use-skeletons boundary stays in both files' contested lists.) — [NN/g skeleton screens, Luke Wroblewski] — [mechanical]
18. **Never stack loading states: one coordinated loading experience per view.** Six independent spinners popping in and out reads as chaos; group loading so the view resolves in one or two visual steps. — [NN/g, Vercel WIG] — [judgment]
19. **Front-load useful content during waits.** Show cached/stale data immediately with a background refresh (stale-while-revalidate) rather than a blank loading state; users prefer slightly old content instantly over fresh content late. — [web.dev/stale-while-revalidate, NN/g] — [judgment]
20. **Make the start of the experience fast even if the total is the same.** Users judge speed by first paint, first interaction, and completion of THEIR task — not by total load. Progressive rendering beats a shorter all-at-once load. — [NN/g perceived performance] — [judgment]
21. **Never freeze the UI during background work.** Saving, uploading, and syncing happen without locking scroll or input; the user keeps working while the app catches up. — [Apple HIG, Material] — [mechanical]
22. **Show upload/download progress with real numbers for long transfers.** Percent, size, or time remaining — indeterminate spinners on multi-second transfers make users assume a hang and retry. — [NN/g progress indicators] — [mechanical]
23. **Debounce expensive reactions to typing, never the echo of typing itself.** Characters must appear instantly; search-as-you-type queries fire debounced behind the keystrokes. Input latency is the most-felt latency there is. — [web.dev/inp, Vercel WIG] — [mechanical]
24. **The pressed state is instant and physical.** Buttons visually depress on touch-down (not touch-up); list rows highlight on touch. Missing active states make a fast app feel unresponsive. — [Apple HIG, Material states] — [mechanical]

## C. Instant navigations and prefetching

25. **In-app navigations feel instant (~under 100-300ms) — never a full white-page reload.** Client-side or prerendered transitions with preserved shell (nav bar, tabs stay put); the browser-style full reload between internal screens marks an app as amateur in 2026. — [web.dev, Next.js/SPA conventions] — [mechanical]
26. **Prefetch likely next destinations — links in view, hover/press intent — so the next screen is already there.** Viewport-based and intent-based prefetch (hover ≈ 200-300ms head start) is standard framework behavior; use it. — [Next.js prefetching, web.dev speculation rules] — [mechanical]
27. **Prefetch politely: never on data-saver, respect metered connections, cap volume.** Prefetching spends the user's data and battery on speculation; honor `Save-Data` and `navigator.connection`, and don't prefetch heavy assets speculatively on cellular. — [web.dev speculation-rules etiquette] — [mechanical]
28. **Back must be instant and restore exactly where the user was.** Scroll position, form state, filter state survive back navigation (bfcache-eligible pages; no `unload` handlers that break it). A back that reloads and scrolls to top loses users' place and trust. — [web.dev/bfcache] — [mechanical]
29. **Cache aggressively; the second visit should be near-instant.** Static assets immutable with hashed filenames; app shell cached; repeat visits should not re-download the world. — [web.dev/http-cache] — [mechanical]
30. **Never block navigation on analytics or tracking.** Beacon/`keepalive` for exit tracking; a link that hangs while a tracker fires is stolen user time. — [web.dev, Vercel WIG] — [mechanical]
31. **Preserve state in the URL so refresh, share, and back/forward all work.** Filters, tabs, search terms, selected items encode into the URL; a refresh that resets the user's context is data loss. — [Vercel Web Interface Guidelines] — [mechanical]
32. **Use view transitions (or equivalent) to make navigation feel continuous, not teleporting.** Shared-element or crossfade transitions between screens carry context; hard cuts between unrelated-looking screens make users re-orient every time. Keep them fast (~150-300ms). — [View Transitions API, Material motion] — [judgment]

## D. Images and media performance

33. **Serve responsive images: right size for the actual rendered slot and screen density.** `srcset`/`sizes` (or a framework image component) so a phone never downloads a 2000px desktop image. Oversized images are the #1 page-weight sin. — [web.dev/serve-responsive-images] — [mechanical]
34. **Serve modern formats (AVIF/WebP) with fallbacks.** 30-60% smaller than JPEG/PNG at equal quality; every major browser supports them in 2026. — [web.dev/uses-webp-images] — [mechanical]
35. **Lazy-load below-fold images; NEVER lazy-load the LCP/hero image.** `loading="lazy"` off-screen, but the hero gets `fetchpriority="high"` and eager loading — lazy-loading it is the most common self-inflicted LCP wound. — [web.dev/lcp-lazy-loading] — [mechanical]
36. **Every image has intrinsic dimensions or an aspect-ratio box.** No dimension = layout shift when it arrives (see #9). — [web.dev/optimize-cls] — [mechanical]
37. **Show a placeholder (blur-up, dominant color, or skeleton) while images load.** Empty white rectangles that pop into photos feel broken; a low-quality placeholder makes the same load feel smooth. — [web.dev, industry standard (Medium/Instagram pattern)] — [judgment]
38. **Video never autoplays with sound; heavy media never loads until needed.** Muted autoplay only, `preload="none"`/poster for below-fold video, and user-initiated playback for anything with audio. — [Apple HIG, Chrome autoplay policy] — [mechanical]
39. **Compress and cap image quality deliberately (q≈70-85), and audit weight per page.** Visually-lossless-enough beats pixel-perfect at 4x the bytes; total image weight is part of the performance budget. — [web.dev image optimization] — [mechanical]
40. **Use CDN-delivered, cache-immutable media.** Media served from origin without long-lived caching wastes every repeat visit. — [web.dev/http-cache] — [mechanical]

## E. Fonts without flashes

41. **Text is never invisible while fonts load: use `font-display: swap` or `optional`.** FOIT (flash of invisible text) hides content for seconds on slow networks; visible fallback text always beats invisible branded text. — [web.dev/font-display, web.dev/avoid-invisible-text] — [mechanical]
42. **Minimize FOUT jank: metric-matched fallback fonts so the swap doesn't reflow.** Tune the fallback with `size-adjust`/`ascent-override` (or a tool that generates them) so swapping to the webfont doesn't shift layout — font swap is a classic hidden CLS source. — [web.dev/css-size-adjust, Vercel/Next font handling] — [mechanical]
43. **Self-host fonts, preload the one or two critical files, and subset them.** Third-party font CDNs add connection cost; unsubsetted fonts ship thousands of unused glyphs. WOFF2 only. — [web.dev/font-best-practices] — [mechanical]
44. **Cap custom font count (roughly 2 families, few weights); use variable fonts for multiple weights.** Every weight is a network file; variable fonts collapse them into one. — [web.dev/font-best-practices] — [mechanical]
45. **System font stacks are a legitimate zero-cost choice for UI chrome.** For app UI (not brand moments), the platform font loads instantly and feels native. — [Apple HIG typography, GitHub/Booking precedent] — [judgment]

## F. Bundle and JavaScript discipline

46. **Ship the least JavaScript that does the job; treat JS as the most expensive byte.** JS costs download + parse + compile + execute on the main thread — 100kB of JS hurts far more than 100kB of image. Budget it separately and hard. — [web.dev/optimize-lcp, Addy Osmani "Cost of JavaScript"] — [mechanical]
47. **Code-split by route and by interaction.** Users download only the screen they're on; heavy widgets (editor, chart, map) load when summoned, with a placeholder reserved so nothing shifts. — [web.dev/code-splitting] — [mechanical]
48. **Audit and evict dependencies regularly; no 50kB library for a 5-line function.** Bundle-analyze on every release; transitive bloat is how apps get slow without anyone deciding it. — [web.dev, bundlephobia norms] — [mechanical]
49. **Third-party scripts are guilty until proven innocent.** Load analytics/chat/AB-test scripts deferred or in a worker (Partytown-style), never render-blocking; measure each vendor's cost and drop those that fail the budget. Tag managers are the top INP killer in the wild. — [web.dev/optimizing-third-party-javascript] — [mechanical]
50. **Tree-shake and dead-code-eliminate as a build guarantee.** Shipping unused exports is paying users' bandwidth for nothing. — [web.dev] — [mechanical]
51. **Compress everything: Brotli (or gzip) for text assets, minified.** Uncompressed text delivery in 2026 is negligence. — [web.dev/text-compression] — [mechanical]
52. **Defer non-critical work to idle time.** Analytics init, prefetch warming, non-visible rendering run via `requestIdleCallback`/after-interactive — never in the critical startup path. — [web.dev] — [mechanical]

## G. Hydration and load discipline (no flash of wrong content)

53. **No flash of wrong content — ever.** No FOUC (unstyled flash), no logged-out header that flips to logged-in, no light-theme flash before dark theme applies, no default language flashing before the user's. Resolve user state server-side or with a blocking inline snippet for the few bytes that matter (theme class). — [web.dev, Vercel WIG] — [mechanical]
54. **Hydration must not eat input: fields keep focus and typed value, taps are not dropped.** If the user types into a server-rendered form before JS attaches, that input survives; frameworks that replay or preserve are configured to do so. — [Vercel Web Interface Guidelines] — [mechanical]
55. **Server HTML and client render must match — zero hydration mismatch.** Mismatches cause visible content swaps, lost state, and console errors; dates/locale/random content are computed once and passed down. — [React/Next hydration docs] — [mechanical]
56. **Interactive elements are never visible-but-dead.** If a button renders before its handler attaches, either it works via native fallback (form post, link) or it doesn't render as enabled yet. A tap that silently does nothing during load destroys trust in every later tap. — [Vercel WIG, progressive enhancement consensus] — [mechanical]
57. **The page is visually stable from first paint: no post-load re-layout.** Client-side measurements that reposition content after render (masonry recalcs, JS-centered modals) are layout shift with extra steps; compute layout in CSS. — [web.dev/cls] — [mechanical]
58. **Cookie banners, toasts, and overlays never cause layout shift or steal focus on load.** They overlay; they don't push; they don't grab keyboard focus from what the user was doing. — [web.dev/cls, WCAG focus consensus] — [mechanical]
59. **Personalized/experimental content resolves before paint or degrades gracefully.** A/B tests that flash variant A then swap to B (client-side flicker) are visible lies; run experiments server-side/edge-side. — [web.dev, Optimizely/VWO anti-flicker consensus] — [mechanical]
60. **Show real content progressively, not a full-screen splash that gates everything.** Full-screen branded splash screens on the web are stolen seconds; stream/render what's ready. (Native-app-style splash is acceptable only in installed PWAs, where the platform shows it.) — [web.dev, Apple HIG launch screens] — [judgment]

## H. Animation and scroll performance

61. **Animate only `transform` and `opacity`; never animate layout properties.** `top/left/width/height/margin` animations trigger layout every frame and jank; compositor-only properties stay at 60/120fps. — [web.dev/animations-guide, Vercel WIG] — [mechanical]
62. **Every animation holds frame rate on a mid-range phone — jank is a defect, not a style.** A dropped-frame animation is worse than no animation; test with DevTools frame profiling on throttled CPU. — [web.dev, Material motion] — [mechanical]
63. **Keep UI animations short: ~150-300ms for transitions, under ~100ms for micro-feedback.** Long animations are repeated taxes; users trigger the same transitions thousands of times. — [Material motion durations, NN/g animation duration] — [mechanical]
64. **Honor `prefers-reduced-motion` everywhere.** Replace movement with fades or cuts for users who've asked for it; vestibular disorders make parallax and zoom physically unpleasant. This is a system setting, not a preference to ignore. — [WCAG 2.3.3, web.dev, Apple HIG] — [mechanical]
65. **Scrolling is sacred: never hijack, slow, or re-easing native scroll.** Scroll-jacking (custom momentum, forced section snapping against user intent, wheel speed changes) is one of the most-hated patterns measured; use native scroll with, at most, CSS scroll-snap where content is truly paginated. — [NN/g scrolljacking, Vercel WIG] — [judgment]
66. **Virtualize long lists; the DOM is not infinite.** Thousand-row lists render a window, not everything; unvirtualized long feeds destroy scroll performance and memory. — [Vercel WIG, react-window/Android RecyclerView convention] — [mechanical]
67. **No scroll-linked effects on the main thread.** Parallax/scroll-progress effects use CSS scroll-driven animations or passive listeners + transforms; a scroll handler that does layout work makes every scroll frame late. — [web.dev scroll-driven animations] — [mechanical]
68. **Animations are interruptible and never block input.** A user who taps mid-transition gets the tap honored; "wait for my animation" is the app putting itself before the user. — [Apple HIG (interruptible animation), Material] — [judgment]

## I. Memory and long-session health

69. **The app survives a long session without degrading.** Hours of use, hundreds of navigations: no growing lag, no crash. Profile for leaks (detached DOM nodes, unremoved listeners, ever-growing stores/caches). — [web.dev memory tooling, Chrome DevTools guidance] — [mechanical]
70. **Unmount = clean up: every listener, timer, observer, socket, and subscription is released.** Leaked intervals and listeners are the standard SPA leak; the discipline is mechanical (effect cleanup, AbortController). — [React/MDN consensus] — [mechanical]
71. **Cap in-memory caches and media retention.** Infinite feeds evict off-screen images/DOM; unbounded client caches eventually kill low-RAM devices first — the users least able to complain. — [web.dev, virtualization consensus] — [mechanical]
72. **Handle tab restore and background-tab discard gracefully.** Mobile browsers discard background tabs constantly; on restore, state comes back (persisted drafts, URL state) rather than a blank reset. — [Page Lifecycle API, web.dev] — [mechanical]
73. **Timers, polling, and animation pause when the tab/app is hidden.** Background polling burns battery and data for a screen nobody sees; use the Page Visibility API. — [web.dev page-visibility, battery consensus] — [mechanical]

## J. Viewport, safe areas, and mobile layout correctness

74. **Viewport meta is correct: `width=device-width, initial-scale=1`, and NEVER `user-scalable=no` or `maximum-scale=1`.** Disabling pinch-zoom is an accessibility violation (WCAG) and Apple/Chrome both penalize it; low-vision users depend on zoom. — [WCAG 1.4.4, Apple HIG, web.dev] — [mechanical]
75. **Respect safe areas: notches, Dynamic Island, home-indicator, and curved corners never cover content or controls.** Use `viewport-fit=cover` plus `env(safe-area-inset-*)` padding; a button under the home indicator is unreachable. — [Apple HIG layout, MDN env()] — [mechanical]
76. **Use small/large/dynamic viewport units correctly; `100vh` is broken on mobile.** Mobile URL bars collapse and expand; `100vh` overflows or leaves gaps. Use `dvh/svh/lvh` intentionally (e.g., `100dvh` for full-screen surfaces) and don't tie layout to a viewport that resizes on scroll. — [web.dev viewport units, MDN] — [mechanical]
77. **Zero horizontal overflow at every width from 320px up.** A sideways-scrollable page on mobile is a broken page; test fluidly across the range, not at one magic width. — [responsive design consensus, WCAG reflow 1.4.10] — [mechanical]
78. **Layouts are fluid, not device-targeted.** Breakpoints follow the content (where the layout breaks), not device model lists; foldables, tablets, and split-screen windows make device-sniffing layouts obsolete. — [responsive design consensus, Material window size classes] — [mechanical]
79. **Support landscape or consciously handle it — never render broken sideways.** If the app is portrait-only, that's a deliberate, communicated constraint (rare on web); otherwise rotation preserves state and layout adapts. Rotation must never lose user work. — [Apple HIG, Material] — [mechanical]
80. **Test with real OS font scaling and display zoom.** Users run 120-200% text size at the OS level; layouts must reflow, not truncate or overlap. — [WCAG 1.4.4, Apple Dynamic Type, Android font scale] — [mechanical]
81. **Full-bleed interactive edges account for OS gesture zones.** Screen-edge swipes belong to the OS (back gesture, app switcher); don't place must-swipe UI in the first ~20px of screen edges where it fights system gestures. — [Apple HIG gestures, Android gesture navigation] — [judgment]

## K. Touch, thumb zone, and one-handed use

82. **Touch targets are at least 44x44pt (Apple) / 48x48dp (Android), with ~8px spacing between them.** Visual size can be smaller but the hit area cannot; cramped targets cause mistaps and rage. — [Apple HIG, Material touch targets, WCAG 2.5.8] — [mechanical]
83. **Primary actions live in the thumb zone: bottom half of the screen, reachable one-handed.** On tall phones the top corners are the hardest real estate; navigation and key actions go bottom (tab bars, FABs, bottom sheets), destructive/rare actions can live top. — [Steven Hoober thumb-zone research, Apple HIG (bottom placement trend), Material] — [judgment]
84. **Provide swipe/gesture shortcuts only as accelerators, never as the only path.** Every gesture-invoked action also has a visible button; gestures are undiscoverable by definition. — [Apple HIG gestures, NN/g] — [mechanical]
85. **Distinguish tap, long-press, and swipe zones so they never collide.** A card that taps to open, swipes to delete, and long-presses for menu needs forgiving thresholds; accidental destructive swipes need undo. — [Apple HIG, Material] — [judgment]
86. **No hover-dependent functionality on touch.** Anything revealed on hover (tooltips, menus, secondary actions) has a touch-visible equivalent; hover does not exist for most of your users. — [web.dev, NN/g] — [mechanical]
87. **Eliminate tap delays and double-firing.** `touch-action: manipulation` where needed; no ghost 300ms delays, no handlers that fire both touch and click. Taps feel wired directly to the response. — [web.dev, MDN touch-action] — [mechanical]
88. **Drag interactions have generous handles and never fight scroll.** Draggables on touch need explicit handles or long-press-to-lift; a list that starts dragging when the user meant to scroll is broken. — [Material drag conventions, iOS reorder pattern] — [judgment]
89. **Design for interruption: mobile sessions are seconds long and constantly abandoned.** Every flow survives an incoming call, app switch, or lock screen mid-way — state is persisted continuously, not on submit. — [Apple HIG, mobile-first consensus] — [judgment]

## L. Keyboards and inputs on mobile

90. **Text inputs are at least 16px font-size on iOS — or Safari zooms the page on focus.** The infamous auto-zoom: any input under 16px makes iOS Safari zoom in and often not zoom back, wrecking the layout. This is a hard floor. — [iOS Safari behavior, universal web consensus] — [mechanical]
91. **A focused field is never hidden under the keyboard.** Scroll the field into view above the keyboard (VisualViewport API / `interactive-widget` handling); typing into an invisible field is a total failure. Test every form with the keyboard actually open. — [web.dev virtual keyboard, Vercel WIG] — [mechanical]
92. **Sticky bottom bars and CTAs account for the open keyboard.** Fixed-bottom buttons either move above the keyboard or get out of the way; a "Continue" button trapped under the keyboard stalls the whole flow. — [web.dev VirtualKeyboard/VisualViewport] — [mechanical]
93. **Every input declares the right keyboard: `inputmode`, `type`, and `enterkeyhint`.** Numeric keypad for codes, email keyboard with @, "Search"/"Go"/"Send" on the return key. The wrong keyboard on every field is death by a thousand cuts. — [MDN inputmode, Apple HIG, web.dev forms] — [mechanical]
94. **Wire up `autocomplete` tokens and never block paste.** `autocomplete="email"`, `one-time-code` for OTP fields (auto-fills from SMS), address tokens for checkout; blocking paste breaks password managers and is user-hostile. — [web.dev sign-in forms, HTML autofill spec, NCSC/NIST guidance] — [mechanical]
95. **Disable autocorrect/auto-capitalize where they sabotage input.** Usernames, emails, codes: `autocapitalize="none" autocorrect="off" spellcheck="false"`. Fighting autocorrect in an email field is a classic avoidable rage. — [web.dev forms, Apple HIG] — [mechanical]
96. **Dismissing the keyboard is easy and never loses input.** Tap-outside or scroll dismisses (`overscroll`/scroll `keyboard-dismiss` behavior); dismissal never clears the field or collapses the form. — [Apple HIG, Material] — [mechanical]
97. **Prefer platform pickers and steppers over freeform typing on mobile.** Date pickers, selects, segmented choices: typing on a phone is the highest-friction input; every field you can turn into a tap is a gift. (Form design details are a sibling file's domain — the mobile-specific point is minimizing keyboard summons.) — [Baymard mobile forms, Apple HIG] — [judgment]

## M. Mobile browser quirks that break UX

98. **Treat `position: fixed` as fragile on mobile — verify it with keyboard open, URL bar moving, and while scrolling.** iOS Safari historically mishandles fixed elements during toolbar collapse, keyboard presence, and momentum scroll; prefer `sticky` where possible and test fixed UI on real devices. — [iOS Safari known behavior, web consensus] — [mechanical]
99. **Control overscroll: no accidental pull-to-refresh, no scroll chaining under modals.** `overscroll-behavior: contain/none` on scrollable panels and modals so an inner scroll at its end doesn't yank the page or trigger Chrome's pull-to-refresh mid-interaction. — [MDN overscroll-behavior, web.dev] — [mechanical]
100. **If you offer pull-to-refresh, follow the platform convention exactly.** Spinner follows the pull, releases at threshold, refreshes newest-first; half-implemented custom pull-to-refresh feels wrong instantly because everyone knows the real one. In feeds it's expected; on static pages disable browser PTR rather than fight it. — [Material pull-to-refresh spec, iOS convention] — [mechanical]
101. **Body scroll locks correctly when overlays open.** Open modal/sheet = page behind must not scroll or rubber-band (a solved-but-fiddly iOS problem — test it); on close, scroll position is exactly where it was. — [iOS Safari scroll-lock consensus] — [mechanical]
102. **Kill iOS's default tap flash and double-tap zoom surprises deliberately.** Set `-webkit-tap-highlight-color` in favor of real active states; ensure interactive elements don't trigger double-tap-to-zoom. — [iOS Safari conventions] — [mechanical]
103. **Momentum scrolling and rubber-banding stay native.** Nested scroll areas keep `-webkit-overflow-scrolling` smoothness; never simulate physics in JS. — [iOS behavior, scroll consensus] — [mechanical]
104. **Test on real iOS Safari and Android Chrome, not just desktop emulation.** DevTools emulation misses keyboard behavior, fixed-position bugs, safe areas, PWA behavior, and GPU differences; the real-device pass is non-negotiable before ship. — [universal mobile-web consensus] — [mechanical]
105. **In-app browsers (Instagram/TikTok/Gmail webviews) are a real environment — verify critical flows there.** A large share of mobile traffic arrives in webviews with quirks (no install prompt, odd viewport, blocked downloads, sometimes broken OAuth); auth and checkout must survive them or detect and offer "open in browser." — [OAuth-in-webview restrictions (Google), industry consensus] — [mechanical]

## N. PWA and installed-app conventions

106. **If the app is installable, meet full PWA baseline: manifest, correct icons (including maskable), theme color, offline fallback.** A broken install (wrong icon, white splash, missing name) is worse than no install. — [web.dev PWA checklist] — [mechanical]
107. **Standalone mode is a different UI contract: no browser chrome means YOU provide back navigation everywhere.** In installed mode there's no URL bar or back button on iOS; every screen needs an in-app way back and external links must open sensibly. — [web.dev PWA, Apple HIG] — [mechanical]
108. **Never spam install prompts; offer install contextually after demonstrated engagement.** Browsers gate the prompt for a reason; an install nag on first visit is a bounce generator. Provide a quiet "Install app" affordance in a menu plus a contextual moment. — [web.dev install promotion patterns] — [judgment]
109. **An installed app works offline at least to a shell-and-message level.** Launching the installed icon with no network must show your UI with a clear offline state — a browser dinosaur inside an "app" breaks the illusion completely. — [web.dev offline UX, PWA baseline] — [mechanical]
110. **Match the OS: theme-color, status bar contrast, splash assets, and dark-mode assets.** The seams between OS chrome and your app should be invisible — mismatched status bar color over your header reads as broken. — [web.dev, Apple HIG] — [mechanical]
111. **Sync state across the installed app and browser tab identities.** Login and data must be the same app in both contexts; users don't know they're "different storage contexts" and shouldn't have to. — [PWA consensus] — [mechanical]

## O. Device respect: battery, data, low-end devices

112. **Honor Save-Data / data-saver mode: lighter images, no speculative prefetch, no autoplay.** The user explicitly asked to spend less data; ignoring the signal is disrespect with a spec name. — [web.dev Save-Data, `prefers-reduced-data`] — [mechanical]
113. **Set a low-end device budget and test on one.** A ~$150 Android with 3GB RAM is the median global device; if the app is unusable there, "works on my iPhone" is a bug report, not a defense. — [web.dev (Alcatel/Moto baseline testing), Addy Osmani] — [mechanical]
114. **Don't burn battery in the background: no busy polling, no always-on GPS/sensors, no constant wake-ups.** Use push instead of poll, batch network calls, stop sensors when unused; users notice which apps drain them and uninstall accordingly. — [Apple HIG energy, Android vitals] — [mechanical]
115. **Continuous GPU effects are a deliberate, budgeted choice — never ambient decoration on every screen.** WebGL scenes, endless canvas loops, and video backgrounds heat phones and drain batteries; run them only where they earn their cost, pause when off-screen/hidden, and provide reduced fallbacks. — [web.dev, Apple HIG energy] — [judgment]
116. **Respect `prefers-reduced-*` signals as a family.** Reduced motion, reduced data, reduced transparency, contrast preferences, forced-colors: system-level accessibility and resource signals are commands, not hints. — [MDN media queries, WCAG] — [mechanical]
117. **Degrade features by capability, not by blocking the browser.** Feature-detect (not UA-sniff); older devices get a working lighter experience, never a "please use Chrome on desktop" wall for capabilities the flow doesn't truly need. — [progressive enhancement consensus, MDN] — [mechanical]
118. **Keep the app usable on flaky networks: design for offline-ish, not just offline.** Timeouts with retry, queued writes that sync later, clear "saved locally / will sync" states; the subway is a normal operating environment, not an edge case. — [offline-first consensus, web.dev reliability] — [judgment]

## P. Privacy UX and honest consent

119. **Consent is a real choice: "Decline" is as easy and as prominent as "Accept."** Equal-weight buttons at the same level; burying reject behind "Manage settings" while Accept is one tap is now explicitly illegal in the EU and called out by the FTC. — [GDPR/EDPB guidance, deceptive.design, FTC dark-patterns report] — [mechanical]
120. **No pre-ticked boxes, no consent by inertia.** Opt-IN for anything non-essential (marketing, tracking, data sharing); pre-checked consent is invalid under GDPR and reads as sleazy everywhere. — [GDPR Planet49 ruling, FTC] — [mechanical]
121. **Say what you collect and why, in plain language, at the moment it matters.** Just-in-time context ("We use your location to find gyms near you") beats a 40-page policy; permission requests come with a one-line reason. — [Apple HIG privacy, NN/g, GDPR transparency] — [judgment]
122. **Ask for permissions in context, after showing value — never a wall of prompts on first launch.** Request notifications/location/camera when the user takes an action that needs it; cold-open permission barrages get reflexive denials and can't be re-asked. — [Apple HIG, Material, web.dev permission UX] — [judgment]
123. **Never use a browser permission prompt as a surprise: pre-prompt with your own explanation.** The OS prompt is a one-shot resource; a soft-ask ("Want game-day reminders?") before the real ask preserves the ability to ask later. — [web.dev notification permission patterns] — [mechanical]
124. **Account deletion is self-serve, findable, and complete.** In-app path, no phone call, no chat-with-retention-agent gauntlet; confirm what's deleted and when. Apple requires in-app deletion; FTC "click to cancel" logic applies. — [Apple App Store guideline 5.1.1(v), GDPR right to erasure, FTC] — [mechanical]
125. **Data export is available and usable.** Users can take their data (standard formats) without begging support; portability is both law (GDPR Art. 20) and a trust signal. — [GDPR, consensus] — [mechanical]
126. **Privacy defaults are the protective ones.** New accounts default private-ish; sharing, discoverability, and tracking expand only by user choice. "Privacy by default" is the legal and ethical baseline. — [GDPR Art. 25, FTC consent decrees] — [judgment]
127. **Don't ask for data you don't need.** Every extra field is friction AND liability; minimum viable data collection is a UX principle, a legal one, and a security one at once. — [GDPR data minimization, Baymard form findings] — [judgment]
128. **Changes to terms/privacy get honest, specific notice — not a silent update.** "We now share X with Y" in a sentence, with a real choice where required; a vague "we've updated our terms" email is trust erosion. — [FTC, GDPR] — [judgment]

## Q. Security UX

129. **Never break password managers.** Standard `<input type="password">`, correct `autocomplete="current-password"/"new-password"`, no paste blocking, no splitting passwords across fields, login fields present in the initial DOM (not injected in ways managers can't see). — [web.dev sign-in form best practices, NCSC/NIST] — [mechanical]
130. **Support passkeys/WebAuthn as the modern default sign-in, with email/password fallback.** Platform authenticators (Face ID/fingerprint) are 2026's mainstream expectation for new apps; offering only passwords is dated, offering only magic links is friction. — [FIDO/WebAuthn adoption, Apple/Google/Microsoft passkey rollout] — [mechanical]
131. **Show password rules up front and validate them live — never reveal requirements only via rejection.** Requirements listed at the field, checked as the user types; and per NIST, length beats composition rules (don't force `P@ssw0rd!` theater). — [NIST 800-63B, Baymard, NN/g] — [mechanical]
132. **Offer show/hide password toggles.** Typos on masked mobile keyboards are endemic; a reveal toggle is standard, safe, and reduces reset traffic. — [NN/g, web.dev sign-in forms] — [mechanical]
133. **2FA/OTP entry is optimized: `autocomplete="one-time-code"`, auto-fill from SMS/authenticator, paste allowed — with auto-submit on complete code retagged [contested].** Six separate boxes that fight paste and autofill turn security into punishment. The auto-submit piece is converged industry practice but sits in direct tension with WCAG 3.2.2 On Input as documented in canon 05 §14.101 (no submit-on-change; auto-ADVANCE between split fields is separately rejected there, and that rejection stands as-is). Auto-SUBMIT needs a house decision; if adopted, it requires visible submission feedback and an error path that restores the field. Offer app-based/passkey 2FA over SMS where possible. — [web.dev OTP, NIST (SMS as weakest factor); tension: WCAG 3.2.2, canon 05 §14.101] — [contested]
134. **Explain security friction when you impose it.** Re-authentication before sensitive actions, new-device verification, session expiry: each says why in one line. Unexplained security walls read as bugs. — [NN/g, banking UX consensus] — [judgment]
135. **Session handling is transparent and controllable.** Show active sessions/devices, allow remote sign-out, "log out everywhere" on password change; silent multi-device sessions are a trust gap. — [OWASP session management, Google/Apple account precedent] — [mechanical]
136. **Session expiry never destroys work.** Timing out mid-form preserves the draft and returns the user to it after re-auth; security that deletes a user's 20-minute effort teaches users to hate security. — [NN/g, OWASP UX boundary] — [mechanical]
137. **Error messages don't leak, but recovery paths don't punish.** "Wrong email or password" without confirming which — while account-recovery flows are prominent, fast, and don't shame. Rate-limit and captcha invisibly before inconveniencing humans. — [OWASP authentication guidance] — [mechanical]
138. **Security-relevant events get notified honestly.** New sign-in from a new device, password changed, email changed — plain notifications with "wasn't you?" paths. Silence around account changes is how users find out via fraud. — [industry consensus (Google/Apple), OWASP] — [mechanical]
139. **All of it over HTTPS with no mixed content — and never train users to ignore warnings.** Certificate errors, http:// forms, and broken padlocks are trust wounds even when "harmless"; the app never asks users to click through a security warning. — [OWASP, browser consensus] — [mechanical]

## R. Payments and transactional trust

140. **The payment step looks and feels more secure than any other screen.** Visual "robustness" around card fields (contained panel, padlock iconography, restrained design); Baymard: perceived security is a gut-feeling judgment made from visual cues, largely independent of actual security. — [Baymard perceived security studies] — [judgment]
141. **Show the total — with shipping, taxes, and fees — before asking for payment details.** Surprise costs at the last step are the #1 measured cause of checkout abandonment; estimate early, itemize clearly. — [Baymard cart-abandonment research] — [mechanical]
142. **Card entry follows the standard anatomy: single card number field with live formatting/brand detection, proper autocomplete tokens, wallet options first.** Apple Pay/Google Pay/PayPal above manual entry; card number spaces itself in groups of four; expiry as MM/YY. Deviating from the anatomy users know creates hesitation exactly where hesitation kills. — [Baymard payment form research, Stripe/checkout conventions] — [mechanical]
143. **Never store or ask for more payment data than the transaction needs, and say how it's handled.** "Your card details are processed by Stripe; we never see them" is one sentence of massive reassurance. — [Baymard, PCI-DSS boundary] — [judgment]
144. **State the price, the billing period, and the renewal terms unmissably before purchase.** "Free trial, then $9.99/month starting Aug 22, cancel anytime" — at the button, not in footnote gray. Obscured auto-renewal is the canonical subscription dark pattern and now FTC-actionable. — [FTC Negative Option Rule, deceptive.design] — [mechanical]
145. **Confirm every transaction immediately on-screen and by email/receipt.** What was charged, to what card, what happens next; a charge with no confirmation triggers disputes and panic. — [Baymard, transactional email consensus] — [mechanical]
146. **Refund and cancellation policies are visible before purchase, not after.** Hiding the return policy until post-purchase is a trust withdrawal that costs more than the returns. — [Baymard trust research] — [judgment]
147. **Errors during payment never double-charge or leave ambiguity.** Idempotent submission, disabled double-click, and an explicit "you have not been charged" / "you were charged once" resolution on failure. Ambiguous payment errors are the single scariest state in software. — [Stripe/payments UX consensus] — [mechanical]
148. **Guest checkout exists; account creation is an offer after purchase, not a toll before it.** Forced registration is a top-three measured abandonment cause. (Checkout flow structure is a sibling file's domain; this is the trust angle.) — [Baymard] — [mechanical]

## S. Anti-dark-pattern consensus

149. **No confirm-shaming.** Decline options are neutral ("No thanks"), never self-insulting ("No, I hate saving money"); this pattern is by-name condemned by FTC, NN/g, and deceptive.design. — [FTC dark-patterns report, deceptive.design] — [mechanical]
150. **No roach motels: leaving any state is as easy as entering it.** Cancel a subscription in as few steps as subscribing (FTC "click-to-cancel"); unsubscribe in one click; downgrade without a phone call. Asymmetric exits are now regulatory targets, not just bad manners. — [FTC Negative Option Rule, deceptive.design] — [mechanical]
151. **Honest urgency and scarcity only.** Countdown timers that reset, fake "3 left!" stock, invented "12 people are looking at this" are deception with legal exposure (EU Omnibus, FTC). Real deadlines and real stock levels are fine — and must be real. — [EU consumer law, FTC, deceptive.design] — [mechanical]
152. **No sneaking: nothing enters the cart, the subscription, or the consent set without explicit user action.** Sneak-into-basket, bundled "protection plans" defaulted on, and silent trial-to-paid conversions are the canonical sneaking patterns. — [deceptive.design taxonomy, FTC] — [mechanical]
153. **No forced continuity without warning.** Free trials that convert to paid notify before the charge (with easy cancel from the notice); charging quietly at trial end is the most-litigated dark pattern of the decade. — [FTC, EU consumer protection] — [mechanical]
154. **No misdirection weighting: visual prominence matches user interest, not business preference, on consequential choices.** Giant glowing "Accept all" next to gray-link "Reject" is interface interference; on choices with consequences (money, data, commitments), options get honest equal weight. — [EDPB, deceptive.design, NN/g] — [judgment]
155. **No nagging loops: a dismissed prompt stays dismissed.** Re-asking for ratings/notifications/upsells every session teaches users the app doesn't respect answers; cap re-asks with long cool-downs and remember "no." — [deceptive.design (nagging), Apple HIG ratings guidance] — [mechanical]
156. **No obstruction of comparison or transparency: prices are findable, plans comparable, and "contact us for pricing" isn't hiding a published number.** Hidden or personalized-without-disclosure pricing erodes trust the moment it's discovered. — [FTC, consumer-protection consensus] — [judgment]
157. **No trick wording: questions are phrased so a fast reader chooses correctly.** No double negatives ("Uncheck to not receive no emails"), no toggles whose direction is ambiguous; the lazy reading and the careful reading must produce the same understanding. — [deceptive.design, NN/g] — [judgment]
158. **No friend spam / contact-harvesting under vague pretenses.** Address-book access asks for a specific stated purpose and never messages contacts without explicit per-action consent. — [deceptive.design, platform policy consensus] — [mechanical]
159. **Ads are unmistakably ads.** Disguised ads (fake download buttons, ads styled as content or as UI chrome) are a named dark pattern and an FTC disclosure violation. — [FTC native-advertising guidance, deceptive.design] — [mechanical]
160. **The product does not weaponize defaults against the user's known intent.** Defaults are set to what a reasonable user would want, not what extracts the most; "default = recommendation" is a trust contract. — [NN/g defaults research, FTC] — [judgment]

## T. Reliability communication and status honesty

161. **Have a public status page and link it from your error states.** When the app is down, users need one authoritative place; "Is it me or them?" resolved in one click preserves enormous goodwill. — [industry standard (statuspage convention), Google SRE] — [mechanical]
162. **Report incidents honestly and promptly — acknowledge before you diagnose.** "We're investigating errors affecting login (since 14:05)" within minutes beats a perfect postmortem hours late; silence during an outage is read as either ignorance or cover-up. — [Google SRE incident comms, statuspage norms] — [judgment]
163. **Distinguish "you're offline," "we're down," and "something in between" in-app.** Detect connectivity and say the truth: an "our servers are having trouble" message when the user's wifi died sends them cursing the wrong party — and vice versa. — [offline UX consensus, NN/g error messaging] — [mechanical]
164. **Announce maintenance ahead of time, schedule it kindly, and honor the window.** Planned downtime with 24h+ notice, off-peak timing, and an accurate end time; "extended maintenance" that overruns silently burns trust like an outage. — [SRE/ops consensus] — [judgment]
165. **Degrade with a banner, not a lie.** If search is degraded or data is stale due to an incident, say so inline ("Results may be out of date") rather than serving wrong data as if fine. — [Google SRE, honest-systems consensus] — [judgment]
166. **After a failure that affected users, close the loop.** Post-incident note in plain language (what broke, what we did); for data-affecting incidents, direct notification. Owning failures publicly is a measured trust builder. — [SRE postmortem culture, breach-notification law] — [judgment]

## U. Resilience and graceful degradation

167. **One component's crash never takes down the screen.** Error boundaries around independent regions: a failed widget shows a contained "couldn't load this" with retry while everything else keeps working. Full-app white-screen from one null is amateur hour. — [React error boundaries consensus, resilience engineering] — [mechanical]
168. **Every fetch has a failure plan: retry with backoff, a human-readable fallback, and a manual retry affordance.** Silent infinite spinners on failed requests are the most common resilience defect; timeout, then tell the user, then let them retry. — [NN/g error UX, SRE patterns] — [mechanical]
169. **Core content works before/without JavaScript where feasible; enhancement layers on top.** Progressive enhancement: links are links, forms can post, content is in HTML. Where the app is unavoidably JS-dependent, a JS failure still shows a real message, not a blank page. — [progressive enhancement consensus, GOV.UK service manual] — [judgment]
170. **Failed optional subsystems disappear gracefully.** If recommendations, chat, or analytics fail to load, the app looks complete without them — no broken placeholders, no console-error cascade, no blocked flows. — [graceful degradation consensus] — [mechanical]
171. **Writes are never lost to a failure.** Drafts persist locally through crashes, refreshes, and network failures; a submit that fails keeps the composed content on screen. "Your message was lost" is the least forgivable error. — [autosave consensus, NN/g] — [mechanical]
172. **Retries are safe by design.** Idempotency on the backend, disabled resubmit on the frontend, so the user's natural "click it again" behavior never duplicates orders, posts, or charges. — [payments/API consensus, Stripe] — [mechanical]
173. **Version skew doesn't strand users.** Long-lived tabs surviving a deploy get a gentle "A new version is available — refresh" rather than mysterious breakage from stale bundles calling changed APIs. — [SPA deployment consensus, Vercel/Next skew protection] — [mechanical]
174. **Third-party outages are contained.** If the map/payment/auth vendor is down, the app explains and offers what still works; a vendor's 500 must not render as your blank page. — [resilience consensus] — [mechanical]
175. **Feature flags and kill switches fail closed to a working experience.** A misfiring flag shows the stable default, never a half-rendered hybrid; the user should never see the seams of your experiment infrastructure. — [feature-flag consensus (LaunchDarkly et al.)] — [mechanical]

## Contested / no-consensus (excluded from the numbered canon)

- **"LCP threshold tightened to 2.0s in a March 2026 update"** — circulating in SEO blogs; web.dev canonical documentation still states 2.5s. Treat 2.5s as the pass line and 2.0s as a competitive aspiration. [contested]
- **Skeleton screens universally beat spinners** — NN/g notes evidence is mixed for very short waits and mismatched skeletons; consensus is only "matched-layout skeletons for content-shaped loads" (as stated in #17). [contested beyond that]
- **Trust seals/badges on checkout for well-known brands** — Baymard shows strong effect for lesser-known merchants; for household-name brands the effect is small and clutter costs may dominate. Judgment call by brand strength. [contested]
- **Bottom navigation vs. hamburger on mobile web** (as opposed to installed apps, where bottom tabs are consensus) — still argued for content sites; app-like products trend bottom-tab. [contested]
- **Service-worker-first offline caching for everything** — full offline-first adds complexity and stale-data risk many teams reject; consensus covers only offline *fallbacks* and installed-PWA shells (#109), not universal offline-first. [contested]
- **Blocking third-party cookies/fingerprinting as "trust UX"** — privacy-positive but implementation consensus (which measurement stack is acceptable) does not exist. [no consensus]
- **AVIF as sole format without WebP/JPEG fallback** — support is near-universal but CDN/tooling edge cases keep fallback the recommended posture. [contested]
- **Haptic feedback on web (Vibration API)** — inconsistent platform support (iOS Safari lacks it); native-app consensus does not transfer to web. [no consensus]
- **OTP auto-submit on complete code (#133)** — converged product practice (web.dev, major sign-in flows) vs WCAG 3.2.2 On Input (canon 05 §14.101 forbids submit-on-change). Retagged [contested] 2026-07-22; needs a house decision. Auto-advance between split OTP fields is NOT contested — the canon rejects it (05 §14.101); a single OTP field with paste support (canon 01 §D.35) sidesteps the whole question. [contested]
